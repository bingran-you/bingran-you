The metastability of lipid vesicle shapes in uniaxial extensional flow
M.A. Shishkin 1,2 and E.S. Pikina 1,3
1Landau Institute for Theoretical Physics of the RAS, Moscow Region, Chernogolovka 142432,

Abstract
In this work, we investigate the elastic properties of deflated vesicles and their shape dynamics in uniaxial extensional flow. By analysing the Helfrich bending energy and viscous flow stresses in the limit of highly elongated shapes, we demonstrate that all stationary vesicle configurations are metastable. For vesicles with small reduced volume, we identify the type of bifurcation at which the stationary state is lost, leading to unbounded vesicle elongation in time. We show that the stationary -
vesicle length remains finite at the critical extension rate. The critical behaviour of the stationary vesicle length and of the growth rates of small perturbations is obtained analytically and confirmed by direct numerical computations. The beginning stage of the unbounded elongation dynamics is simulated numerically, in agreement with the analytical predictions.
Introduction
Vesicles are volumes of fluid enclosed by a molecularly thin lipid bilayer. These fluid containers play a critical role in countless biological processes, including the storage and transport of nutrients [1], cellular signalling [2], and exocytosis [3]. In recent years, GUVs (giant unilamellar vesicles) have become a canonical model system for the much more complex, protein-rich membranes of living cells [4–6]. Moreover, lipid vesicles have found widespread application in various technological fie-
lds [4, 7], including their use as biocompatible drug-delivery capsules [8, 9]. In these applications, a fundamental issue is the determination of vesicle shape dynamics in response to external influences.
The main physical properties of lipid bilayers have been established in numerous experimental studies [4, 10]. First, the bilayer behaves as a weakly compressible liquid film, because there is an equilibrium surface concentration of molecules. In addition, due to the low permeability of the bilayer to most molecules in the surrounding fluid, the vesicle volume is fixed by the condition of zero osmotic pressure difference [11]. The leading contribution to the surface free energy density arising fro-
m shape deformations of a vesicle can be expressed in terms of local radii of curvature and is known as the Helfrich energy in the physics literature [11], or equivalently as the Willmore energy in
mathematics [12]. The equilibrium shape of a vesicle in the absence of hydrodynamic motion is determined by minimising the bending energy at fixed surface area S and enclosed volume V . Due to the scale invariance of the Helfrich energy, the equilibrium shape is fully characterised by the dimensionless reduced volume V = V /VS ≤ 1, where VS = 4/3π(S/(4π))3/2 is the volume of a sphere with surface area S [13–15]. The coexistence of two conserved quantities, area and volume, leads to non-spherical e-
quilibrium shapes, such as prolate, oblate, discocyte, and stomatocyte forms [10].
In recent years, the non-equilibrium dynamics of vesicles in external flows has become a subject of intensive research [4, 16], relevant to both microfluidic devices and biological systems. Most studies have focused on vesicle dynamics in steady shear flows, revealing three distinct dynamical regimes: tumbling, trembling, and tank-treading. Theoretical models [17–22] show excellent agreement with experimental observations [23–29] and numerical simulations [30–35].
The behaviour of vesicles in extensional flow, however, is less well understood. In 2008, Kantsler et al. studied the dynamics of strongly deflated vesicles (V < 0.56) in a planar extensional flow [36] and discovered the existence of a critical strain rate above which vesicles undergo unbounded elongation. In this regime, the vesicle shape consists of two nearly spherical balls connected by a narrow tubular neck. At significantly

Extensive numerical studies of highly elongated vesicles in axisymmetric extensional flow were later performed in [37, 38]. The dynamics of vesicles with moderate elongation in extensional flow was investigated numerically in [39] and subsequently in [37]. It was shown that vesicles transition to unbounded elongation through the development of an asymmetric instability, where -
the final balls differ in size. This transition occurs only for sufficiently deflated vesicles with V < Vc ≈ 0.75, while less deflated vesicles become more spheroidal as the strain rate increases. Experimental studies of this asymmetric instability were reported in [40, 41]. More recently, the development of feedback-based techniques for holding a vesicle near the flow focus (Stokes traps) has enabled detailed investigations of vesicle dynamics in planar extensional flows [42, 43], as well as rela-
xation after strong stretching [44]. Further progress in this area involved studying vesicle dynamics in mixed flows [28, 45], where it was shown that the critical strain rate increases with the magnitude of the shear component. When the strain rate varies in time [46, 47], an additional characteristic timescale emerges, leading to three dynamic regimes for weakly deflated vesicles: pulsating, reorienting, and symmetric. 1.1 Dissipative Equations
In the hydrodynamic limit, lipid bilayers are treated as infinitely thin fluid films of arbitrary shape immersed in a viscous fluid. This approach assumes that characteristic shape variations occur on length scales much larger than the bilayer thickness (1–3 nm), which is valid for typical vesicle experiments [36]. We study the time evolution of the vesicle shape. Hereafter, the terms membrane and lipid bilayer are used interchangeably.

2.1 General Properties of a Stretched
Vesicle
Since at equilibrium the Helfrich energy (2) is minimised for fixed vesicle volume and surface area, elongation is associated with an additional contribution to the Helfrich energy and, correspondingly, a restoring force. In this section, we derive the scaling of this force with the vesicle length; in fact, this exposition generalises the derivation in [36] to the case of strong stretching. First, we note that if one attempts to stretch a cylinder while preserving both its volume V ∼ LR2 and surfa-
ce area S ∼ LR, it cannot remain cylindrical inevitably, thinner and thicker regions will appear. The cylinder is constrained from elongating by its fixed volume. The Helfrich energy of a tube of length L and radius R can be estimated as Fκ|tube ∼ κL/R; if we instead express the radius through the tube surface area Stube ∼ LR, we have:

From this, we conclude that the lowest-energy configuration for a strongly stretched vesicle is one in which the tube contains as much area as possible, while the volume is contained in other regions. At the same time, the tube area can only decrease during stretching, since some area is required to form volume reservoirs. We note that as elongation proceeds, the tube volume decreases, Vtube ∼ St2ube/L; therefore, asymptotically, the volume reservoir takes the shape that encloses the entire volume-

Conclusion
In this paper, we study the shape dynamics of vesicles in an axisymmetric extensional flow. Refining the analysis of the Helfrich elastic energy and the viscous stress from the flow for elongated configurations presented in [36], we establish the metastable nature of any stationary vesicle state in an extensional flow and estimate the extent of the stability region.
For vesicles with small reduced volume V (corresponding to large ratios of semi-axes at equilibrium), this analysis allows us to determine the type of bifurcation at which a stationary state disappears at a critical flow strain rate, leading the vesicle to undergo unbounded elongation.
