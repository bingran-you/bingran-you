# Zero-Human vs One-Person Company

For a few months I treated these as a clean either-or — &quot;one-person company is the legal thing, zero-human company is the operating thing.&quot; That framing didn&apos;t survive the way the terms are actually being used in 2026. Both are operating models. The legal Indian OPC is a separate, much narrower meaning of the same words. And the two operating models are not opposites; they sit on a spectrum, with the boundary fuzzy and shifting.

Here is how I read the relationship now.

## They are both operating models

A [one-person company](/one-person-company), in the dominant 2026 sense, is a founder who orchestrates AI agents — Claude Code, Codex, OpenClaw, Cursor, plus specialised agents for research, growth, support, and ops — together with SaaS and APIs and selective outsourcing, to run a complete business solo. Coinbase has explicitly begun building &quot;one-person teams&quot; inside the company under the same logic. Sam Altman and Dario Amodei have publicly predicted a one-person billion-dollar company. Medvi&apos;s reported $401M of 2025 sales is the most cited proof-of-existence case (with caveats — it later hired family and contractors, and the chatbot failure modes are public).

A [zero-human company](/zero-human-company) is the high-leverage end of that spectrum. The founder retreats further, from operator to owner, board, and policy setter. An *organisation* of agents — not a single agent — handles daily operations through a control plane (org chart, budgets, heartbeats, governance, audit). Paperclip is the canonical open-source implementation. Andon Labs&apos; Andon Market and Anthropic&apos;s Project Vend are the live experiments. ClawBank and Coinbase x402 / AWS Bedrock AgentCore Payments are the rails layer.

The legal sense — the Indian One-Person Company under Companies Act, 2013 — is a corporate form for a single shareholder. It says nothing about how the work gets done. A founder running a zero-human company can be incorporated as an OPC, an LLC, a C-corp, or anything else. Pick the legal form for tax and liability; pick the operating model for execution.

## The spectrum, in one read

Roughly:

- **Solopreneur / freelancer.** One human personally does the execution work. Bottleneck: hours.
- **Indie hacker.** One human ships a product, leans on no-code and SaaS, light agent assist. Most of the public &quot;solo founder&quot; stories.
- **AI-native one-person company (the 2026 sense).** One human as orchestrator of an AI labour stack. Day spent on direction, taste, approvals.
- **[Zero-human company](/zero-human-company).** One human at governance only. Organisation of agents at execution. Control plane (Paperclip-style) is the actual product surface.

The boundary between the last two moves every quarter as agent reliability, tool permissions, payment rails, and control-plane software improve.

## Why the conflation hurts

Two real-world failure modes I&apos;ve seen.

**Founder reads &quot;zero-human company&quot; literally and tries to retreat to governance before the agent organisation is reliable.** They end up with a stack that drifts, contradicts itself, blows budget, or ships brand-damaging output. The fix is not to give up; it is to operate honestly at the AI-native one-person company mode, where the founder is still the orchestrator, until the control plane is good enough to step further back.

**Founder treats one-person company as a legal-form question and incorporates an Indian OPC because they read about &quot;zero-employee companies.&quot;** The OPC&apos;s nominee-director and conversion rules then bite into a structure that was supposed to stay simple. Wrong question, wrong answer.

The first founder needed an honest assessment of the operating model and a Paperclip-style control plane. The second founder needed a flexible legal entity (LLC) and a clear-eyed view of which mode they actually run.

## A working vocabulary, restated

For this site, I will keep using the terms as follows:

- **One-Person Company (legal sense)**: a corporate form with a single shareholder, e.g. the Indian OPC. A question for your lawyer.
- **[One-person company (operating-model sense)](/one-person-company)**: any company actually run by one human, with the centre of gravity in 2026 firmly on the AI-native end — founder as orchestrator of an agent stack.
- **Solopreneur**: a low-leverage one-person company where the human personally does the execution work.
- **[Zero-human company](/zero-human-company)**: the high-leverage endpoint — an organisation of agents running daily operations under a control plane, with a human at governance. As of 2026, the working examples are honestly described as human-supervised autonomous operations.

If you are pitching, building, hiring, or critiquing in this space, picking the right term saves you a lot of crossed wires.
