Abstract
Purpose Osteonecrosis of femoral head (ONFH) is a common joint disease with high morbidity in mainland China. We performed a retrospective case investigation based on the widest range of patients ever to reveal the demographic and clinical characteristics of ONFH in mainland China. Methods We collected data from hospitalized patients of nine large tertiary hospitals in China, focusing on the aetiology of ONFH. Medical records and self-design questionnaires were used to collect data. We determined -
the patients’ aetiology according to established guidelines and categorized patients into different subgroups. We further analysed the underlying illness of the steroid-induced osteonecrosis of femoral head. Results A total of 6,395 patients with complete medical records were included in the study. The age (mean±SD) of the patients was 46.45±13.80 years. Males dominate with 70.1 %
of all ONFH patients. In all the ONFH patients, 24.1 % are steroid induced, 30.7 % are alcoholic, 16.4 % are traumatic and 28.8 % are idiopathic. Among steroid-induced osteonecrosis of femoral head patients, 546 patients have autoimmune disorder (46.63 %), which is the most common underling illness, among these, the most common disease is systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE). Conclusions The most common age when ONFH occurs is between 40 and 50 years old. Male patients developed ONFH 3.4 years earli-
er than female patients. The number of the male patients is greater than the female, reaching the overall gender proportion of 7:3. Autoimmune disease is the primary cause of steroid-induced ONFH, among which SLE is the most common disease.

Introduction
Osteonecrosis of the femoral head (ONFH) is a group of very common diseases. The total number of ONFH in the world is estimated to be 20,000,000. The prevalence rate in the United Sates is about 15,000∼20,000 [1, 2]. The morbidity in Korea is 28.9/100,000 and in Japan 8.9/100,000 [3, 4]. This disease results in very high rate of disability, thus bringing inestimable labour and economic loss to the patients’ families and the whole society throughout the world [5, 6]. Ju et al. [7, 8] reported in 20-
09 that the number of ONFH in China is about 5,000,000–7,500,000. Liu et al. reported [9] in 2007 that there were 100,000–200,000 new cases in China every year. Investigation of a large population over wide regions of China to reveal the latest situation of ONFH is in urgent need. Only a few studies have shown the constitution of different aetiologies in a countrywide population, and these few constitutional data varied a lot from country to country. Fukushima et al. [4] reported the constitution -
of different aetiologies among non-traumatic ONFH in Japan, where 51% were steroid induced, 31% alcoholic ONFH, and 15 % idiopathic. Kang et al. reported [3] that the etiological constitution of all ONFH in Korea is steroid induced (14.6 %), alcoholic ONFH (32.4 %), idiopathic (51.4 %) and traumatic (1.6 %). Some of the etiologies have strong correlation with lifestyle and may demonstrate different distribution patterns in different regions. So knowing the current aetiological constitution in Chin-
a is necessary and valuable to establish proper preventive strategies and help decrease the morbidity of this disease. In this study, we focused on ONFH patients in large hospitals in mainland China and conducted a retrospective case investigation based on the widest range of patients ever to reveal the demographic and clinical characteristics of ONFH. Since there is no large registry study on ONFH in Mainland China, a proper substitute is needed to compare the demographic characteristics and natu-
ral history with western patients; a reliable and representative data on ONFH could be helpful for public health and local government.
Materials and methods
This study is a retrospective cross-sectional study approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Peking Union Medical College Hospital. During the research, participants’ medical
records were de-identified and randomly re-numbered to protect their privacy. The patients’ original complete medical record, including the paper versions, are all stored in the record room of each hospital. Collected data were summarized and stored in PUMCH only for research purposes. We selected nine tertiary hospitals in mainland China, the patients from nine hospitals were from different regions and different income levels; we hoped this could reflect the population characteristics. We extract-
ed the medical records of all hospitalized adult patients diagnosed as osteonecrosis of femoral head (ONFH) from January 2010 to December 2013 in the nine tertiary hospitals (Peking Union Medical College Hospital, PUMCH; Beijing Jishuitan Hospital, JST; No 3. Hospital of Peking University, BYSY; Renmin Hospital of Peking University, BDRM; China-Japan Friendship Hospital, ZRYH; Huaxi Hospital of Sichuan University, HX; No.1 Hospital of Zhejiang University, ZHYEY; No. 1 Hospital of Guangzhou Univers-
ity of Traditional Chinese Medicine, GZHY and No.1 Hospital of Xinjiang Medical College, XJ). The inclusion criteria were as follows. All the patients were diagnosed as ONFH based on guidelines written in 1995 [10]. The severity of the disease was determined based on the Ficat staging [11, 12]. To avoid confounding factors, we excluded the following patients: (1) hip joint tuberculosis (which has totally different aetiology from femoral head necrosis), (2) hip joint necrosis after infectious disea-
ses, (3) congenital dysplasia of hip, (4) arthritis caused by systemic diseases, (5) Kaschin-Beck disease. We built self-designed survey forms to collect general condition, past medical history, medication history, symptoms, physical examination findings, clinical diagnoses, radiological and surgical information through computerized medical record systems of nine hospitals. In our study, ONFH patients were categorized as traumatic, steroid induced, alcoholic and idiopathic based on medical history-
. The traumatic ONFH was defined as patients, who had history of femoral neck fracture, or dislocation of hip joint, fracture of acetabulum, with or without history of internal fixation surgery. We further divided non-traumatic ONFH into steroid induced, alcoholic and idiopathic ONFH. First, patients with clear description about steroid usage (standard therapy>two months, or steroid impulse therapy) and dosage [peak dose >40 mg prednisone, or average dose >20 mg prednisone, or cumulative dose >2,0-
00 mg prednisone], or mention of a large amount of steroid usage in medical records were considered as steroid induced ONFH. Second, the alcoholic ONFH is defined if a clear description could be found in medical records of over 400 ml

(or 320 g) alcohol [13] each week or other kind of liquor with the same alcohol content, without femoral neck fracture history or high dose steroid-using history. Lastly, patients with no medical records of steroid usage, alcohol intake, history of fracture, or any other causative factors were considered as idiopathic ONFH.
Statistical analysis
All normal distribution data were reported as mean±SD. All non-normal distribution values were reported as median (range, 25th and 75th percentiles). Component ratio was reported in percentile. Ratio differences were tested using χ2test. A two-tailed p value of <0.05 was considered to be of statistical significance. All analyses were performed using SPSS 21.0 (SPSS Inc. Chicago, IL, USA).
Results
ONFH proportion in the THA
Among all the ONFH patients in the nine medical centres, 5, 920 of them had the operation of total hip arthroplasty (THA), while the rest of them accepted a hip preserving operation. There were a total of 19,427 patients undergoing THA in the nine medical centers over four years (2010–2013), of which 30.5 % were because of ONFH.
Demographic characteristics of the ONFH patients in mainland China
A total of 6,395 were included in the clinical analysis with complete medical history. Among them, 4,466 (69.8 %) were male and 1,929 (30.2 %) were female, with a gender ratio of 2.3:1. The mean age at the time of diagnosis was 46.5±13.8 years (male 45.4±12.8; female 48.8±15.6). The mean age difference was 3.4 (2.67-4.14). The age distribution is demonstrated in Fig. 1.
Trend of ONFH onset in patients from different regions
We calculated and developed a heat-map for patients of different ages, and analysed the trends of ONFH onset in patients from different regions. We found the average age of onset was slightly greater in more developed provinces than that in less developed areas (Fig. 2).
Aetiological distribution of ONFH in mainland China
We retrospectively analysed the few studies which reported the aetiological distribution of ONFH in mainland China (Fig. 3). The ratio of steroid-induced ONFH increased significantly from 1990 to 2010, while the traumatic ONFH decreased during the same period. However, the ratio of alcoholic ONFH did not show any obvious trends. Though the trend was the result of a rough analysis, it did have its clinical significance and indications.

Aetiological characteristics of ONFH patients in mainland China
Of all the patients, there were 1,543 steroid induced ONFH patients (24.1 %), 1,962 alcoholic ONFH patients (30.7 %), 1,046 traumatic ONFH patients (16.4 %) and 1,844 idiopathic ONFH patients (28.8 %). Gender was associated with different aetiologies. In male patients, alcoholic was the most common aetiology (40.4 %); while in female patients, idiopathic and steroid induced were the major aetiologies (38.3 % and 32.9 %). There were much less alcoholic female ONFH patients (5.9 %) (Fig. 4). The rat-
ios of different aetiologies varied among different hospitals (Table 1). There were 1,543 steroid induced ONFH patients, all of which had detailed descriptions of steroid usage and 1,171 of them had clear records of underlying illness. Among all the steroid-induced patients, 55.7 % of them had long-term
regular usage (>6 months), 17.8 % with long-term (>6 months) intermittent usage, and 26.5 % were with shortterm (<6 months) large-amount (>300 mg/day) usage (including steroid pulse therapy). A total of 61 underlying illnesses of steroid-induced ONFH were collected (Table 2). Autoimmune diseases were the major primary cause, which covered nearly half the patients (n=482, 41.16 %). Other major primary causes included dermatological diseases (n = 118, 10.08 %), respiratory diseases (n=115, 9.82 %), -
nephritic diseases/nephritis (n=95, 8.11 %), hematological diseases (n=77, 6.58 %), neurologic diseases (n=58, 4.95 %), craniocerebral trauma and brain tumour (post-operative use) (n=57, 4.87 %), eye diseases (n=35, 2.99 %), and organ transplantation (n=34, 2.90 %). Among all the autoimmune diseases causing ONFH, systemic lupus erythematosus was the most often seen major cause (217 patients, 18.53 % of all steroid induced ONFH).

Alcohol abuse as a causative factor of ONFH
Among ONFH patients with alcohol abuse, the average time of alcohol intake history was 22.4±10.0 years, the average amount was 1781.5±1127.0 g/week, whereby a total of 30 patients had more than 3500 g/week.
Discussion
ONFH is a common disease of the hip joint with relatively frequent disability. Much progress has been accomplished about clinical manifestation and surgical therapy [14–16]; however, characteristics of natural history and epidemiology of ONFH remain unclear, especially in mainland China. This study, to our knowledge, is the largest medical centre-based survey in mainland China, aiming to investigate the ratio of ONFH potential causative factors. Our study enrolled nine large tertiary hospitals in -
different areas of China, including a total of 6,395 cases of inpatients ONFH cases. Among all the patients who underwent THA, Coventry et al. [17] reported that about 18 % are caused by ONFH in western countries. But in our study, ONFH patients accounted for as high as 30.5 % of patients who underwent THA. Previous studies also showed that more ONFH patients accepted THA instead of hip preserving operations, and the ratio increased from 76 % in 1990 to 88 % in 2010. Another possible reason is tha-
t improper steroid usage is more common in less developed areas in China, thus causing more steroid-induced ONFH patients. We noticed the gender ratios were different among the centers, and the ratios also vary in different age groups. This may explain the differences of the causative factor ratios reported by previous studies, for they might have different causative factor ratios as they studied different groups of patients. Patients of the nine medical centres were from 24 provinces of China (a -
total of 34), and according to the heat-map analysis, patients from less developed areas tended to have earlier onset of ONFH. This may be because there is more improper steroid usage in less developed areas due to the imbalance of medical resources distribution in China. Patients from less developed areas may also tend to have larger amounts of alcohol intake. Lastly, people from less developed areas were more engaged in manual labor, thus having higher risk of hip-related trauma. We believe that-
 knowing the ratio of different causative factors would be helpful for improving preventive strategies.

Previous studies reported various kinds of causative factors [18], including steroid usage [19–21], alcohol intake [22–24], trauma [25], etc. However, there were only a few studies about ratio of causative factors of ONFH in mainland China from 1990–2013, all of which were single-centre reports with relatively small sample size (n=104–1143). Our results about causative factor ratios have provided important and fundamental data. Our review of previous reports indicated that the ratio of steroid-in-
duced ONFH increased. This might because that, with the development of diagnosis and treatment of steroiddependent disease such as connective tissue disease, steroids are more widely used nowadays. Further studies are necessary to confirm our observations and reveal the possible mechanisms. According to our survey, we noticed that the ratio of causative factors varies in each centre and is strongly related to age proportion and gender ratio. We also noticed that the common causative factors were u-
sually recorded in the medical records, while the others factors that are relatively less common were not, e.g. coagulopathies, hemoglobinopathy, hyperlipidemia, slipped capital femoral epiphysis, malignant fibrous histiocytoma, etc. Therefore, a more thorough classification with a cause-specific therapeutic strategy would be required in the future. Steroid-induced ONFH is one of the most important parts of ONFH. A large range of diseases requires steroid usage, and connective tissue disease cover-
ed 41 % of all causes. Although systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) has low prevalence and incidence (23.8/100,000 and 7.3/100,000, respectively) [26], it was the top causative disease of steroid-induced ONFH. It is believed that patients with certain underlying illness (e.g. SLE) have higher probability to suffer from ONFH while using steroid treatment. Kenzora reported that the occurrence rate of ONFH is lower in patients who have use of steroid because of head trauma, while the rate is much high-
er in patients with concurrent systemic disease, like SLE, vasculitis, etc. [27]. Further research is needed to reveal the mechanism of this phenomenon. In our study, a total of 61 diseases were recorded to be causative diseases of steroid usage, which suggested that a wide range of patients with steroid usage could be the high risk population of ONFH, and it should be noted that some less common diseases requiring steroids may also cause ONFH, such as systemic sclerosis, nervous tinnitus, myelody-
splastic syndrome, etc. In our study we found 55.7 % of all steroid-induced ONFH patients had a history of long-term regular use of steroids, and 17.8 % with long-term intermittent usage.
A recent comment on Lancet stated that Btaking a break^ in the course of steroid usage may lower the risk of ONFH [28], which indicates that the pattern of steroid usage, instead of cumulative dosage, might be more important. Furthermore, an intermittent usage, instead of long-term continuous usage, might be helpful for preventing ONFH. We believe there was much more improper steroid use in China than in western countries, especially in less developed areas. It is important to put the steroid usag-
e under common standards and conduct further research about the relationship between steroid use pattern and risk of ONFH. There are several limitations in our study. First, the nine centres were all tertiary hospitals in different regions; the subtype of the patients and characteristics of the disease might be different from the primary hospitals and clinics. Second, we set a definition for different causal factors based on previous studies. However, there are still debates and discussion on the -
standard criteria. Some authors recommended that a history of taking over 1800 mg prednisolone over one month or equivalent dosage or a history of corticosteroid use for at least two months could be defined as steroid-induced ONFH. And intake of alcohol over 400 mL per week is considered alcohol induced ONFH [13]. There were reports that even less alcohol intake or lower dose of corticosteroids could increase the risk of ONFH. Future population research is needed to obtain the data for morbidity, -
and large cohort studies are needed to verify the potential risk factors.
Conclusion
The most common age when ONFH occurs is between 40 to 50 years old. Male patients developed ONFH 3.4 years earlier than female patients. The gender ratio is about 7:3, and 30.5 % of all patients who take total hip arthroplasty were because of ONFH. Among all the aetiologies, trauma, steroid usage and alcohol intake are the three most important causes, while the autoimmune disease is the primary cause of the steroidinduced ONFH, accounting for nearly half of the patients.
