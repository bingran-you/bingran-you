TWO-PHOTON 3D LITHOGRAPHY: MATERIALS AND APPLICATIONS
Stephen M. Kuebler,a† Kevin L. Braun,a Francesco Stellacci, a§ Christina A. Bauera, Marcus Halik,a Wenhui Zhou,a Tianyue Yu b* Christopher K. Ober,b Seth R. Marder,a,c and Joseph W. Perrya,c
aDepartment of Chemistry, University of Arizona, Tucson, AZ 85721 USA bDepartment of Materials Science and Engineering, Cornell University, Ithaca, NY 14853 USA cSchool of Chemistry and Biochemistry, Georgia Institute of Technology, Atlanta, GA 30332-0400 USA
Introduction Two-photon lithography is powerful emerging approach to the fabrication of 3D micro- and nano-structures with a
minimum feature size of 120 to 200 nm.i,ii,iii We have reportediv on conjugated organic compounds with large two-photon absorption cross sections that can undergo efficient photochemical processes, such as photoinitiated radical generation,1 photoreduction of metal ionsv and photo-acid generation.vi Using these compounds, high-sensitivity two-photon active polymeric resists (positive and negative tone) and metal nanoparticle based composite materials have been developed. These materials allow for -
laser driven fabrication of 3D polymeric microstructures or the direct writing of 3D metallic structures within a polymer matrix. The completely free-form capability of two-photon lithography allows for the 3D fabrication of a wide range of periodic and non-periodic structures.

Microfabrication by Two-Photon Initiated Radical Generation

We have reported on the design of organic molecules featuring large two-photon absorption cross sections that are of

the form D-π-D, A-π-A, D-π-A-π-D, and A-π-D-π-A, where π represents a π-conjugated backbone, and D and A are
electron donors and acceptors.4 Many electron rich D-π-D two-photon absorbers show two-photon cross sections of ~1,000 x 10-50 cm4 s photon-1 and are also effective two-photon photoinitiators for the radical

polymerization of acrylate monomers. Under CW mode-locked

excitation with 100 fs laser pulses, these chromophores are

substantially more sensitive than that for conventional UV absorbing

radical photoinitiators. Photocrosslinkable resins based on triacrylate

polymer blends containing such two-photon radical generators have

been developed and used to fabricate complex 3D microstructures, such

as the microchain structure shown in Figure 1, by two-photon lithography.1,3 There are support structures on the left and right that

anchor the microchain to the surface, but the five chain links are

connected only topologically to the supports or to one another. The links in the middle of the structure are freely moveable. Such a structure would be very difficult to fabricate by conventional

photolithography, as it would require a layer-by-layer process and

portions of the written structure would be washed away in intermediate development steps.

Positive Tone Two-Photon Microfabrication using a Two-Photon Photoacid Generator

There has been interest in the ability to activate a variety of controlled chemical reactions by two-photon excitation.

As a variety of reactions, including polymerization and

cleavage reactions, can be performed using protons or

Lewis acids, we have developed a two-photon-activatable

photoacid generator, based on a bis-

[(diarylamino)styryl]benzene core with covalently attached sulfonium moieties.6 This photoacid generator has both a
large two-photon absorption cross-section (δ = 690 × 10-50 cm4 s photon-1) and a high quantum yield for the

photochemical generation of acid (φH+ = 0.5). Under nearinfrared laser irradiation, the molecule produces acid subsequent to two-photon excitation and initiates the polymerization of epoxides at an incident intensity that is

one to two orders of magnitude lower than that needed for conventional UV-sensitive initiators. We have utilized this

photoacid generator in conjunction with a positive-tone chemically amplified resist, for the fabrication of a threedimensional buried microchannel structure, as shown in Figure 2.6vii

The array of 12 channels was fabricated at a depth of 8 µm below the surface and the channels were 4 µm in diameter and 50 µm in length. The channels provide subsurface interconnections between two large trench structures that are open at the top surface of the polymer. These openings provide a route for removal of dissolved polymer from the exposed regions below the surface during the development process. The fabricated channels allow for flow of fluids below the surface and we have demonstrated -
the filling of the channels with solvents as well as with liquid crystals. Such structures may find applications in microfluidic devices or, when filled with a suitable electro-optic or nonlinear optical material, as switchable buried optical diffractive devices.

Direct Laser Writing of Metal Features in Two-Photon Activatable Nanocomposites

Means for the direct laser writing of metal is of considerable interest for a variety of applications in electronics,

photonics and micro-electromechanics. We have pursued a nanocomposite approach to develop materials systems that allow for the laser writing of conductive metal features in a polymer matrix using one-photon or two-photon excitation.5

The nanocomposite material is comprised of dispersed ligand-coated metal nanoparticles, a soluble metal salt, an electron-

deficient two-photon absorbing dye, and a plasticizer in a polyvinylcarbazole host.5viii To

achieve an homogeneous dispersion of metal

nanoparticles in the matrix, self-assembled

coatings based on a mixture of alkylthiol ligands

of different lengths and an alklythiol with a

terminal carbazoyl group have been used. The use

of such particles allowed the preparation of optical

quality thin films of nanocomposite material.

Excitation of the dye leads to photoreduction of metal ions and the generated atoms undergo diffusion in the matrix leading to growth of the

Conclusions Compounds with large two-photon absorption cross sections and high photochemical reaction efficiencies have
enabled the development of highly sensitive materials systems for two-photon 3D lithography. A variety of materials patterning chemistries have been demonstrated and these methods pave the way for the fabrication of micro- and nanostructures based on diverse designs and materials compositions. We have demonstrated the fabrication of a representative set of microstructures based on the three classes of two-photon photoactive materials compositions described above. Such structures can be used direct-
ly for suitable applications but can also be used as templates for the growth or molding of other materials as need for a specific application. Given the ability of the two-photon lithography to produce 3D patterns of arbitrary structure, there is potential for the fabrication of an essentially limitless number of micro- and nano-structures in a wide variety of materials compositions.
