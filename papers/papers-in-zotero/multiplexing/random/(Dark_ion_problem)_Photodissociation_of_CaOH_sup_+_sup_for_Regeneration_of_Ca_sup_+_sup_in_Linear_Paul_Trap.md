(Received July 2, 2005; accepted October 19, 2005; published online February 8, 2006)
The photodissociation of trapped CaOHþ produced by CaþÃ(3d 2DJ) þ H2O reaction was performed in order to regenerate Caþ in a linear Paul trap. Most Caþ ions were successfully regenerated from molecular ions by deep UV light irradiation. The photon energy threshold in the CaOHþ þ h  ! Caþ þ OH reaction was estimated to be $4:9 eV. The dissociation energies of XHþ ! Xþ þ H and XOHþ ! Xþ þ OH (X ¼ Be, Mg, Ca) were calculated using standard quantum chemistry codes to search for the possibility of rege-
nerating light alkaline earth ions from molecular ions. The present experiment suggests that the photodissociation method is applicable to the regeneration of alkaline earth ions lost by chemical reactions with typical residual molecules in an ion trap experiment. 1. Introduction
Laser-cooled alkaline earth ions in an ion trap are important research objects for quantum computer prototypes,1) as tools for the sympathetic cooling of molecular ions,2–4) and for a study of non-neutral plasma physics.5,6) Generally, trapped alkaline earth ions hardly react with typical residual molecules, since the molecules have dissociation and ionization energies higher than the kinetic energy and electron aﬃnity of the trapped ions. However, laser excitation can induce certain chemical reac-
tions with such molecules, which results in the loss of objective ions. The ion loss by the laser-induced processes for alkaline earth ions has not been studied suﬃciently. Only a few examples of such processes have been studied.7,8) Recently, the reactivity of laser-excited CaþÃ in 3d 2DJ (J ¼ 3=2, 5/2) states has experimentally been investigated, and the acceleration of a CaþÃ þ H2O reaction has been demonstrated.9)
On the other hand, the chemical reactions of alkaline earth ions with typical residual molecules are closely related to experiments that use radioactive ion beams. In our proposed project, an ion trap combined with an rf ion guide will be used for cooling and trapping energetic ion beams to study the Bohr–Weisskopf eﬀect of unstable alkaline earth ions.10–12) One of the serious problems in optical spectroscopy for unstable isotope ions is the induction of a chemical reaction with residual molecule-
s during the cooling process in the rf ion guide. In general, the collision-induced dissociation (CID) technique is applied to solve this. However, a high-pressure noble gas must be loaded to perform CID. Since a high vacuum is necessary for the optical spectroscopy of trapped ions, photodissociation is a promising method of regenerating target ions from reactionproduct ions instead of CID.
In this paper, we report on the experimental studies of the CaþÃ þ H2O reaction and the photodissociation of trapped CaOHþ ions produced by the reaction. We also report on reaction enthalpies at 0 K associated with the Caþ þ H2O reaction, which were calculated using standard quantum

4. Photodissociation of CaOHþ
The reaction-product CaOHþ ions, as well as residual Caþ ions, can be trapped simultaneously in the ion trap, since the trapping parameters for these ions are in the stable region. If the photodissociation of CaOHþ þ h  ! Caþ þ OH is carried out, the recovery of the LIF of trapped Caþ ions is observed. The calculated dissociation energies D0 of XOHþ ! Xþ þ OH (X ¼ Be, Mg, Ca) are shown in Table II.20) The dissociation energy of CaOHþ ! Caþ þ OH is estimated to be 4.59 eV, which corresponds to the -
