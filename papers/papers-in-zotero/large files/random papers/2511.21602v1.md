1 Introduction
Let (Sn)n≥0 be a discrete-time simple random walk (SRW) on Z starting at S0 = 0. For any n ∈ N and s ∈ Z, write
ξ(s, n) = #{0 ≤ j ≤ n : Sj = s}, R(n) = {s ∈ Z : ∃ i ∈ [0, n], Si = s},
for the local time (number of visits) at site s up to time n, and the range (set of visited sites) of the random walk by time n respectively. Let gk(n) be the number of sites visited exactly k times up to time n, that is,
gk(n) := #{s : ξ(s, n) = k}.
Among these, g1 is particularly interesting and has been the focus of significant research interest. Intuitively, the set of once-visited sites can be linked to the “points of increase” of the SRW (see Dvoretzky, Erdős and Kakutani [5] and Peres [18]). An interesting result was established by Newman [16], who proved that E g1(n) = 2 for all n ∈ N. Furthermore, Han [12] proved the convergence E[gk(n)] → 2 for any fixed k ≥ 2. 1
