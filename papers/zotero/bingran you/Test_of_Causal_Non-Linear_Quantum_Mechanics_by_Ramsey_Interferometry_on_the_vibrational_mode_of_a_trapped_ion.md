Test of Causal Non-Linear Quantum Mechanics by Ramsey Interferometry on the vibrational mode of a trapped ion
Joseph Broz, Bingran You, and Hartmut Haeffner Department of Physics, University of California, Berkeley, California 94720, USA and Challenge Institute for Quantum Computation, University of California, Berkeley, CA 94720
David E. Kaplan and Surjeet Rajendran Department of Physics and Astronomy, The Johns Hopkins University, Baltimore, Maryland 21218, USA
(Dated: April 28, 2022)
Kaplan and Rajendran have recently demonstrated that non-linear and state-dependent terms can be consistently added to quantum field theory to yield causal non-linear time evolution in quantum mechanics [1]. Causal non-linear theories have the unavoidable feature that their quantum effects are dramatically sensitive to the full physical spread of the quantum state of the system. As a result, such theories are not well tested by conventional atomic and nuclear spectroscopy. By using a wellcontrolle-
d superposition of vibrational modes of a 40Ca+ ion trapped in a harmonic potential, we set a stringent limit of 2 × 10−12 on the unitless scaling factor ϵ˜γ of the predicted causal, non-linear perturbation.

Introduction.– It is a basic feature of quantum mechanics (QM) that the wave function be described by a linear equation of motion. This is treated as axiom in deriving many elementary results, like conservation of probability or the no-cloning theorem, essential for a physically selfconsistent theory. But, surprisingly, linear time evolution has never been pinned down as a necessary condition for any such result, raising the possibility that QM is an over-constrained description of reality.
This is evidenced by a number of successful non-linear generalizations of QM (NLQM) that have been independently fleshed out in the literature [2–5]. These examples demonstrate that state-dependent terms can be added to the non-relativistic Schr¨odinger Hamiltonian while maintaining a compatible physical interpretation and correspondence with linear QM. The rigorous experimental tests that have followed [6–11] further highlight the seriousness of these proposals and have also imposed stringent bou-
nds on their predictions.
But the significance of these results was challenged when it was shown that the underlying theories generally violated another key tenet of physics: causality [12, 13]. Evidently, it is the linearity of time evolution that ultimately prevents the nonlocal quantum correlations in an entangled system from being leveraged for nonlocal communication in standard QM. This has led to a persistent notion that linearity is the expression of causality in the Schr¨odinger equation [14]. However, Polchinski h-
as since described a restricted class of non-linear observables [15] that also preserve causality under reasonable assumptions [16]. Thus, causality seems to be yet another physical requirement for which linear time evolution is a sufficient but not necessary condition.
A causal theory.– Recently Kaplan and Rajendran have built on Polchinski’s work by developing a compatible (and thus, causal) NLQM framework [1], which is also capable of handling relativistic systems. A nov-

elty of their approach is the promotion of the non-linear modification from a purely mathematical abstraction to a completely physical (albeit unusual) interaction. This is done by starting with a given (relativistic) QFT Hamiltonian and then pairing each existing interaction with an additional term obtained by replacing the participating field operators with their expectation values. In essence, this is a systematic procedure for reintroducing an element of classical field theory into the fully q-
uantum picture. However, when applied to low-energy systems the generic result is a non-linear Schr¨odinger equation.
For example, the time evolution of single particle with charge q and Hamiltonian H is described in the causal theory by:

Experiment.– Experiments were performed using a single 40Ca+ ion confined inside of a radio-frequency (RF) Paul trap in a parameter regime where the center of mass motion is well modelled as a three-dimensional anisotropic harmonic oscillator (Fig. 1a). The trap is characterized by the natural vibration frequencies along its three axes νx ≈ 2π × 1.01 MHz, νy ≈ 2π × 2.52 MHz and νz ≈ 2π × 2.79 MHz.
The internal state of the ion is manipulated by resonantly coupling well-focused laser beams to various electronic transitions, as illustrated in Fig. 1b. The shortlived 2S1/2 ↔ 2P1/2 and 2D5/2 ↔ 2P3/2 dipole transitions are used for entropy-altering operations like cooling. Measurement, in particular, is performed via the electron shelving method on 2S1/2 ↔ 2P1/2 and allows us to determine the population of the 2S1/2 manifold [21]. For coherent operations, narrowband light at 729 nm is used to co-
uple two Zeeman sublevels of the metastable 2S1/2 ↔ 2D5/2 quadrupole transition, whose degenaracy is broken by application of a strong, static magnetic field. We call this our qubit transition and reference it as |S⟩ ↔ |D⟩.
To prepare the ion in a well-defined state, we first Doppler cool its temperature down to several hundred microkelvin and then optically pump its electronic state into |S⟩. Afterwards, resolved sideband cooling is applied along the x-direction, driving the axial vibrational mode into its ground state with high probability. Once this process is complete, the ion is measured to be in the state |S, nx = 0⟩ with a confidence greater than 99%, where nx refers to the zero-phonon Fock state of the vibrat-

From the ion’s perspective, a laser pointing along one of its vibrational axes will appear to be phase modulated by motion along that direction. By simply detuning the laser from the qubit frequency by an amount equal to +νx, this effect can be used to couple the states |S, n⟩ ↔ |D, n + 1⟩, which we refer to as blue sideband transitions (Fig. 2c) [23]. The energy of the blue sideband transition is already sensitive to the non-linear perturbation and, in principle, can be used for our Ramsey experi-
ment. But the electronic states are first-order sensitive to ambient magnetic field fluctuations and therefore have Q-factors an order of magnitude less than that of the vibrational mode – limiting the length of the coherence window during which the experiment can be performed.
Instead we first map the desired Ramsey superposition onto the ion’s internal electronic states by resonantly driving the qubit transition for a fixed duration, generating the state (α1|S⟩ + α0|D⟩)|0⟩ in an appropriate rotating frame. Here α0 = sin(θ) and α1 = cos(θ) and the value of θ is controlled by adjusting the intensity of the addressing laser.
