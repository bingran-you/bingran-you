HYPERBOLICITY OF ADJOINT LINEAR SERIES ON VARIETIES
WITH POSITIVE TANGENT BUNDLE
ATSUSHI ITO, JOAQU ́IN MORAGA, DEBADITYA RAYCHAUDHURY, AND WERN YEONG
Abstract. Let X be a smooth projective variety of dimension n ≥ 3, and let L be an ample line bundle on X. In this article, we study the algebraic hyperbolicity of a very general section of the adjoint linear series |KX + mL| when the tangent bundle TX of X has suitable positivity properties. As a consequence, we show that the linear system |KX + mL| is hyperbolic (or pseudo-hyperbolic) for m ≥ 3n + 1, for various classes of polarized pairs (X, L), thus providing new evidence of a conjecture that -
was proposed by the second and fourth authors. Moreover, when X is abelian, we show that the linear system |mL| is hyperbolic for m ≥ n, and the same holds when m ≥ n − 1, if |L| has no base divisors. It turns out that these bounds for abelian varieties are sharp. We also prove analogous statements for Kummer varieties and certain classes of hyperelliptic varieties.

1. Introduction
Kobayashi hyperbolicity is a notion of hyperbolicity depending on a pseudo-metric associated to any complex manifold (see [30]). Kobayashi hyperbolic manifolds are important classes of complex manifolds on which the Kobayashi pseudo-metric is indeed a metric. For instance, Kobayashi hyperbolic manifolds admit no non-constant maps from the complex line C. There are several other related hyperbolicity notions such as Brody and arithmetic hyperbolicity, see [26] for a survey. In [17], Demailly introd-
uced the notion of algebraic hyperbolicity for a smooth projective variety X as an algebraic analogue of Kobayashi hyperbolicity (see Definition 2.1). The algebraic hyperbolicity condition imposes a uniform lower bound on the genus of curves C ,→ X which is linear in terms of the degree of C with respect to some fixed polarization on X. It is expected that (very) general hypersurfaces of large degree on smooth projective varieties are hyperbolic. There are many theorems incarnating this principle.-

Motivated by Fujita-type conjectures, the second and fourth authors have recently made the following
Conjecture 1.1 ([35, Conjecture 1.1]). Let X be a smooth projective variety of dimension n ≥ 3, and let L be an ample line bundle on X. Then the linear system |KX + mL| is hyperbolic if m ≥ 3n + 1.
This conjecture was confirmed in [35, Theorem 1.2] for smooth toric varieties as well as in some cases of Gorenstein toric varieties (see, e.g., [35, Theorem 1.3]). More recently, it was studied for general Fano threefolds with Picard number one [45]. We phrase our results in terms of nef values of polarized pairs whose definition we now recall. Given a polarized smooth projective variety (X, L) of dimension n, its nef value is defined as follows
τ (L) := min {t ∈ R | KX + tL is nef} .
It is known that τ (L) ≤ n + 1, and it is ≤ n unless (X, L) ∼= (Pn, OPn (1)) (Maeda). We refer to [2] for a comprehensive literature regarding the structures of polarized algebraic varieties with large nef values.
Our first result is for regular varieties with very ample polarizations:
Theorem 1.2. Let X be a smooth regular projective variety of dimension n ≥ 3 and let L be a very ample line bundle on X. If the tangent bundle TX is nef (resp. pseudo-nef, almost nef ), then the linear system |KX + mL| is hyperbolic (resp. pseudo-hyperbolic, almost hyperbolic) for m > max {n, n + 2τ (L) − 2}.
We refer to Definition 2.2 for the various variants of the hyperbolicity notion used in the above statement. Observe that the number n + 2τ (L) − 2 ≤ 3n, whence the above proves Conjecture 1.1 for any regular variety with nef tangent bundle for very ample polarizations.
In Corollary 2.9 we show that the tangent bundle of a smooth projective Fano variety is pseudo-nef. As an immediate consequence we obtain
Corollary 1.3. Let X be a smooth projective Fano variety of dimension n ≥ 3 and let L be a very ample line bundle on X. Then the linear system |KX + mL| is pseudo-hyperbolic for m > max {n, n + 2τ (L) − 2}.
It is known that projective (almost) homogeneous spaces have (almost) nef tangent bundle (see Section 2 for the definitions). We have an “almost homogeneous variant” of the above, see Corollary 5.1.

Among the classes of varieties with nef tangent bundles, some special ones are the classes of abelian varieties, or more generally homogeneous spaces, and finite  ́etale quotients of abelian varieties (i.e., hyperelliptic varieties). Let us start by discussing the hyperbolicity property of adjoint linear systems on abelian varieties.
It is now well-established that sheaves on abelian varieties enjoy remarkable regularity properties. The study on this topic dates back to Green–Lazarsfeld’s generic vanishing condition [20], which was subsequently studied by Hacon [22] and extended vastly by Pareschi–Popa [40, 41, 43]. The notion of M-regularity emerged through these works, which was extended to Q-twisted setting by Jiang–Pareschi [28]. Our next result is a criterion for hyperbolicity of the linear system |L| through M-regularity-

Theorem 1.9. Let X be an abelian variety of dimension g ≥ 3 and let L be an ample line bundle on X. If Io⟨ 1
g−1 L⟩ is M-regular, then L is very ample and hyperbolic.
As an immediate consequence, we obtain the following
Corollary 1.10. Let X be an abelian variety of dimension g ≥ 3 and let L be an ample line bundle on X. Then the following statements hold:
(1) The linear system |mL| is hyperbolic for m ≥ g. (2) If |L| has no base divisor, then |mL| is hyperbolic for m ≥ g − 1.
It is natural to wonder how optimal the above corollary is. In fact, recall that Conjecture 1.1 is motivated by Fujita-type conjectures, and for polarized abelian varieties (X, L), we have base-point freeness (resp. the very ampleness) of |mL| as soon as m ≥ 2 (resp. m ≥ 3) by Lefschetz theorem. Also observe the following:
Remark 1.11. If L is an ample and base-point free line bundle on a simple abelian variety X of dimension g ≥ 3, then the linear system |L| is hyperbolic. Indeed, as X is simple, it does not contain any abelian proper subvarieties, therefore Theorem 1.4 implies that |L| is hyperbolic.
In view of the above discussions, for polarized abelian varieties (X, L), it is quite tempting to wonder if there is a constant lower bound on m for which the linear system |mL| will satisfy hyperbolicity in every dimension. However, this expectation turns out to be false:
Proposition 1.12. For every g ≥ 2, there exists a polarized abelian g-fold (X, L) such that the linear system |(g − 1)L| is not hyperbolic.
Note that Lazarsfeld’s conjecture, proven by Pareschi [39] shows us that (g − 1)L is projectively normal if g ≥ 4, and satisfies (Np) if g ≥ p + 4 (see for e.g., loc. cit. for the definitions). Hence (Np) does not imply algebraic hyperbolicity even if p is sufficiently large unless we fix the dimension.
We also note that by the above proposition, the statement in Corollary 1.10(1) is indeed optimal. The above also shows that Corollary 1.10(2) is optimal for g = 3, since there exists an abelian threefold with an ample line bundle L such that |2L| (which is base-point free as mentioned before) is not hyperbolic.

2. Preliminaries on hyperbolicity and positivity
In this section, we recall some preliminary definitions regarding algebraic hyperbolicity, positivity properties of sheaves, and their Q-twisted variants.
2.1. Algebraic hyperbolicity. We start with the definition of (Demailly) algebraic hyperbolicity.
Definition 2.1. We say that a projective variety X is algebraically hyperbolic if there is some ε > 0 and ample line bundle L such that every non-constant map f : C → X from a smooth projective curve C of genus g(C) satisfies the inequality
(2.1) 2g(C) − 2 ≥ ε deg f ∗L.
We remark that the algebraic hyperbolicity condition does not dependent on the choice of polarization, i.e., it holds true for L if and only if it holds true for any ample line bundle. Indeed, if H is another ample line bundle on X, then there exists δ > 0 such that L − δH is ample. So the definition works for the pair (H, εδ) provided that it works for (L, ε).
It is reasonable to define hyperbolicity of the linear series associated to an ample and base-point free line bundle through that of a very general section of the series:
Definition 2.2. Let E be an ample and base-point free line bundle on a polarized projective variety (X, L). The line bundle E (or the linear system |E|) is called:
• Hyperbolic if the variety DE in X defined by the vanishing of a very general element of |E| is algebraically hyperbolic. • Pseudo-hyperbolic if there is a proper closed subset Z ⊊ X and a constant ε > 0 such that a very general element DE of |E| is algebraically hyperbolic outside of Z, namely every non-constant map f : C → DE from a smooth projective curve C where f (C) ̸⊂ Z satisfies (2.1). • Almost hyperbolic if there is a countable family of subvarieties Zi ⊊ X (i ∈ N) and a constant ε > 0 s-
uch that a very general element DE of |E| is algebraically hyperbolic outside of S
i∈N Zi, i.e., every
non-constant map f : C → DE from a smooth projective curve C where f (C) ̸⊂ S
i∈N Zi satisfies (2.1).
It turns out that the above definitions do not depend on the polarization L, i.e., one can define hyperbolicity of a line bundle and its variants without specifying any polarization. Moreover, one can also define algebraic pseudo- (resp. almost) hyperbolicity of a projective variety X in a similar fashion. Note that X (or a line bundle E on X) is
hyperbolic =⇒ pseudo-hyperbolic =⇒ almost hyperbolic.
Example 2.3 (Pseudo-hyperbolic but not hyperbolic variety). Note that the converse to the first implication above does not hold. Indeed, take any smooth projective variety that is algebraically hyperbolic. If we blowup a point, the resulting variety is algebraically pseudo-hyperbolic (outside the exceptional divisor) but not algebraically hyperbolic.

2.2. Positivity of (Q-twisted) sheaves and geometry. We recall the definitions of the positivity properties that will be useful. Given a sheaf E on a projective variety, we denote Proj(Sym•(E)) by P(E).

Recall that a variety X is called homogeneous (resp. almost homogeneous) if Aut0(X) acts transitively (resp. with an open orbit) on X. It is well-known that if a smooth projective variety is homogeneous (resp. almost homogeneous), then its tangent bundle TX is nef (resp. almost nef). We also have the following structure theorem for varieties with nef tangent bundle:
Theorem 2.7 ([7, Proposition 2.4]). Let X be a smooth projective variety with nef tangent bundle TX . Then its Albanese map albX : X → A is a surjective and smooth morphism whose fibers are connected with nef tangent bundles.
It has been proven in [37] that the tangent bundle of a smooth Fano variety is generically nef (defined as in [44, Definition 2.1.(1)]). Using that, we proceed to show that it is pseudo-nef.
Proposition 2.8. Let X be a smooth Fano variety. Then there is a proper closed subset Z ⊂ X containing every non-movable curve in X.
Proof. Since X is Fano, the pseudoeffective cone Eff(X) is finitely generated by [3, Corollary 1.3.1] and [23, Proposition 1.11]. Let E1, . . . , Ek be effective divisors on X whose classes generate Eff(X). We will show that every non-movable curve C is contained in E1 ∪ · · · ∪ Ek. By [5, Theorem 0.2], the pseudoeffective cone Eff(X) is dual to the closure Mov(X) of the cone of movable curves. Hence, if C is not movable, then we must have [E] · C < 0 for some effective divisor E. We may write [E]-
 = r1[E1] + ... + rk[Ek] for some non-negative rational numbers r1, . . . , rk. Then [E] · C < 0 implies that we must have [Ei] · C < 0 for some i = 1, . . . , k. Therefore we must have C ⊂ Ei. □
Corollary 2.9. The tangent bundle TX of a smooth Fano variety X is pseudo-nef.
Proof. By Proposition 2.8, the non-movable curves are contained in a proper closed subset Z ⊊ X. The result then follows from [37, Theorem 1.4], which proves that TX |C is nef when C is movable. □
