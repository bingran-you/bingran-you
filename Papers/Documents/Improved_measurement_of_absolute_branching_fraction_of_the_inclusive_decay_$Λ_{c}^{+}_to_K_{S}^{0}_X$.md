Abstract: By analyzing 4.5 fb−1 of e+e− collision data accumulated with the BESIII detector at center-of-mass energies ranging from 4599.53 MeV to 4698.82 MeV, we report the measurement of the absolute branching fraction (BF) of the inclusive decay Λc+ → K0
SX
using the double-tag technique. The result is B(Λc+ → K0
SX) = (10.9 ± 0.2 ± 0.1)%, where the first uncertainty is statistical and the second is systematic. This result indicates that there are still undiscovered decay channels containing K0
S in the final state with a combined
BF of (3.1 ± 0.4)%. The BF of the inclusive decay Λc+ → K0/K0X is calculated to be
B(Λc+ → K0/K0X) = (21.8 ± 0.4 ± 0.2 ± 1.1)%, where the third uncertainty accounts for a possible difference between B(Λc+ → K0
SX) and B(Λc+ → K0
LX). The result is in agreement with the prediction of the statistical isospin model.

1 Introduction
Charmed baryons serve as an excellent laboratory for understanding the properties of quantum chromodynamics (QCD) in the context of a heavy quark coupling with two light quarks. The ground state, Λc+, was first observed by the MARKII experiment in 1979 [1]. In recent years, the BESIII Collaboration has reported a series of absolute branching fractions (BFs) of exclusive decays of Λc+ [2–14]. These provided significantly improved BF values for the known decay modes, and some new decay modes were al-
so discovered. The sum of the observed and predicted BFs of Λc+ is approximately 90% [15, 16]. The measurements of the BFs of the inclusive decays of Λc+ are important for understanding its decay mechanisms and inferring the extent of the undiscovered decays. The Cabibbo-favored (CF) decays are the dominant decay modes of Λc+ [17, 18]. According to the statistical isospin model, the sum of the BFs of both observed and predicted CF decays of Λc+ is (83.2 ± 4.9)% [16], mainly involving Λ, Σ, Ξ and K-
0 in the final state. The inclusive BFs are useful for calibrating the CF transition amplitude for Λc+ and are particularly essential for the determination of the Λc+ lifetime [18, 19]. In 2014, the BESIII experiment accumulated e+e− collision data at the center-of-mass
energy √s = 4599.53 MeV corresponding to an integrated luminosity of (586.9 ± 0.1 ± 3.9) pb−1, which initiated many studies on the inclusive decay of Λc+ [20–22]. In 2020, the BF of Λc+ → K0
SX was measured for the first time, giving the result (9.9 ± 0.6 ± 0.4)% [22], where X means all possible final state particles. The statistical isospin model estimates the total BF of exclusive Λc+ decays containing K0/K0 to be (21.7 ± 0.8)%, as presented in Table 1. However, the summed BF of all observed exclusive Λc+ decays containing
K0/K0 only accounts for a total of (15.5 ± 0.7)%. The determination of the absolute BF of Λc+ → K0/K0X is an important input for the search for unmeasured decay modes of Λc+ and for testing the BFs predicted by the statistical isospin model.

2 BESIII Experiment and Monte Carlo Simulation
The BESIII detector [27] records symmetric e+e− collisions provided by the BEPCII storage ring [28] in the center-of-mass energies range from 2.0 GeV to 4.95 GeV, with a peak
luminosity of 1.1 × 1033 cm−2s−1 achieved at a center-of-mass energy of √s = 3.77 GeV. The cylindrical core of the BESIII detector covers 93% of the full solid angle and comprises a helium-based multilayer drift chamber (MDC), a plastic scintillator time-of-flight system (TOF), and a CsI(Tl) electromagnetic calorimeter (EMC), which are all enclosed in a superconducting solenoidal magnet providing a 1.0 T magnetic field. The solenoid is supported by an octagonal flux-return yoke which is segmented -
into layers and instrumented with resistive plate counter modules for muon identification. The charged-particle momentum resolution at 1 GeV/c is 0.5%, and ionization energy loss dE/dx resolution is 6% for electrons from Bhabha scattering. The EMC measures photon energies with a resolution of 2.5% (5%) at 1 GeV in the barrel (end cap) region. The time resolution of TOF barrel region is 68 ps, while that in the end cap region was 110 ps. The end cap TOF system was updated in 2015 using multigap res-
istive plate chamber technology, providing a time resolution of 60 ps [29–31]. About 85% of the Λc+Λ−
c pairs are produced in data taken after this upgrade. More details can be found in Ref. [27, 28]. High-statistics Monte Carlo (MC) simulated data samples produced with geant4based [32, 33] software, which includes the geometric description of the BESIII detector and the detector response, are used to determine detection efficiencies and estimate backgrounds. The e+e− annihilation is simulated with the kkmc generator [34] incorporating the initialstate radiation (ISR) effects and the beam energy s-
pread. The inclusive MC sample includes the Λc+Λ−
c events, D(∗)
(s) production, ISR return to lower-mass ψ states, and continuum
processes e+e− → qq (q = u, d, s). All the known decay modes of charmed hadrons and charmonia are modeled with evtgen [35, 36] using BFs taken from the PDG [15], while the remaining unknown decays are modeled with lundcharm [37]. In addition, exclusive DT signal MC events, where the Λ−
c decays into the studied ST channels and the Λc+ decays into K0
SX, are used to determine the DT detection efficiencies. For the MC production of
Λc+Λ−
c , the Born cross sections are taken into account [24], and phase space generated Λc+ decays are reweighted according to their observed distributions.
3 Event Selection
Charged tracks detected in the MDC are required to be within a polar angle (θ) range of |cos θ| < 0.93, where θ is defined with respect to the z axis, which is the symmetry axis of the MDC. The distance of the closest approach of the track to the interaction point (IP) must be less than 10 cm along the z axis, and within 1 cm in the transverse plane of the z axis. Both the time-of-flight system and the specific ionization energy loss (dE/dx) in the
–3

MDC are used to determine the likelihoods L of different particle type hypotheses. Tracks are identified as protons when the particle identification (PID) determines this hypothesis to have the greatest likelihood (L(p) > L(K) and L(p) > L(π)), while charged kaons and pions are identified based on comparing the likelihoods for the two hypotheses (L(K) > L(π) or L(π) > L(K)). These criteria are not required for the tracks that are used to reconstruct
K0
S or Λ. Neutral showers are reconstructed in the EMC. Showers not associated with any charged track are identified as photon candidates if they satisfy two additional criteria: (1) an energy deposition in the EMC of Edep > 25 MeV in the barrel region corresponding to the polar angle |cos θ| < 0.8, while Edep > 50 MeV in the end-cap region corresponding to 0.86 < |cos θ| < 0.92. (2) the EMC time difference from the event start time is required to be less than 700 ns to suppress electronic noise and-
 showers unrelated to the event. The π0 candidates are reconstructed from photon pairs with the requirement that their invariant masses lie within 115 MeV/c2 < M (γγ) < 150 MeV/c2. To improve the momentum resolution, a mass-constrained kinematic fit to the π0 nominal mass is applied to the photon pairs, and the updated energy and momentum of the π0 are used for the further analysis. Candidates for K0
S and Λ are formed by combining two oppositely charged tracks into the final states π+π− and pπ+. For these two tracks, their distances of closest approaches to the IP must be within ±20 cm along the beam direction. No distance constraints in the transverse plane are required. The charged pion candidate is not subjected to the PID requirements described above, while PID for the proton is implemented to improve the quality of the signal. The two daughter tracks are constrained to originate from a c-
ommon decay vertex, and the χ2 of the vertex fit is required to be less than 100. Furthermore, the decay vertex is required to be separated from the IP by a distance of at least twice the fitted vertex resolution. The fitted momenta of the π+π− and pπ+ are used in the further analysis. To select K0
S and Λ candidates, we impose the requirements 487 MeV/c2 < M (π+π−) < 511 MeV/c2 and 1111 MeV/c2 < M (pπ+) < 1121 MeV/c2, respectively, which are within about three standard deviations of their mass resolutions in the MC samples. The Σ0 and Σ− candidates are reconstructed with γΛ and pπ0 with invariant masses in 1179 MeV/c2 < M (γΛ) < 1203 MeV/c2 and 1176 MeV/c2 < M (pπ0) < 1200 MeV/c2, respectively. For the tag modes pK0
Sπ0, pK0
Sπ−π+, and Σ−π−π+, possible backgrounds with Λ → pπ+ are rejected by requiring M (pπ+) outside the range (1110, 1120) MeV/c2. In addition, in the mode pK0
Sπ0, candidate events within the range 1170 MeV/c2 < M (pπ0) <
1200 MeV/c2 are excluded to suppress Σ− background. To remove K0
S candidates in the modes Λπ−π+π−, Σ−π0, and Σ−π−π+, the invariant masses of π+π− and π0π0 pairs are not allowed to fall in the range (480, 520) MeV/c2. 5 Systematic Uncertainty
The systematic uncertainties arising from the ST side mostly cancel in the BF measurement according to Eq. 4.2. The systematic uncertainties from various sources are summarized in Table 6 and discussed below.
• K0
S reconstruction.
As described in Section 4, J/ψ → K0
SK±π∓ decay is used as a control sample to determine the correction to the DT efficiencies of (1.8 ± 0.3)%. The systematic uncertainty in the K0
S reconstruction is 0.3%.
• 2D fitting. The uncertainty arising from the simultaneous 2D fitting is estimated by convolving the signal MC shape with a double Gaussian function to vary the signal shape, while the background from e+e− → qq is described using the shape extracted from e+e− → qq MC samples. The total uncertainty from the 2D fitting is 0.9%.
• Intermediate BF. The BF of K0
S → π+π−, Bint = (69.20 ± 0.05)% [15], gives an uncertainty of 0.1%.
• MC statistics. The statistical uncertainties of ST efficiencies and DT efficiencies are propagated to the BFs of signal channel according to Eq. 4.2, and contribute with an uncertainty of 0.3%.
• ST Λc− yield. The systematic uncertainty in the total ST yield arises from the background fluctuation together with a component coming from the fit to the MBC distribution. It is studied by varying the parameters of signal shape, fitting range and endpoint of the ARGUS function, and then redoing the fit process. This gives an uncertainty of 0.4%.
• Rdp⃗ requirement. The uncertainty arising from the Rdp⃗ is estimated by obtaining the BF without this requirement, leading to an uncertainty of 0.1%.

6 Summary
The absolute BF of Λc+ → K0
SX is measured based on about 4.5 fb−1 of e+e− collision data collected at center-of-mass energies ranging from 4599.56 MeV to 4698.82 MeV with the BESIII detector. The result is B(Λc+ → K0
SX) = (10.9 ± 0.2 ± 0.1)%, where the first uncertainty is statistical and the second is systematic. The precision of the measured BF is improved by a factor of three compared to the previous BESIII measurement B(Λc+ →
K0
SX) = (9.9 ± 0.6 ± 0.4)% [22]. Compared to the summed BF of the observed exclusive Λc+ decays, (7.8 ± 0.3)%, our result indicates that the combined BF of the undiscovered decay channels of Λc+ that include a K0
S meson in the final state is (3.1 ± 0.4)%. The BF of the
inclusive decay Λc+ → K0/K0X is calculated to be (21.8±0.4±0.2±1.1)%, where the third uncertainty accounts for a possible difference between B(Λc+ → K0
SX) and B(Λc+ → K0
L X ), as reported in Ref. [23]. This result is consistent with the prediction from the statistical isospin model, B(Λc+ → K0/K0X) = (21.7 ± 0.8)%. The total predicted BF of unseen
decays by the statistical isospin model of the inclusive decay Λc+ → K0/K0X is (6.2±0.4)% as listed in Table 1. This is also consistent with the BF of the undiscovered decay channels of Λc+ containing K0
S in the final state, estimated from our measured result. It can be seen from Table 1 that the predicted undiscovered decay channels are dominated by the decays which contain neutrons in the final state. Due to the challenge of neutron reconstruction, only the BF of Λc+ → nK0

of the unobserved decays of Λc+ → K0/K0X, could be measured. For Λc+ → Ξ0K0π+, the theory based on SU(3) flavor symmetry predicts its BF to be (8.7 ± 1.7)% [41], which differs significantly from the estimation by the statistical isospin model, as listed in Table 1. Our result disfavors the predictions of SU(3) flavor symmetry indirectly and shows that a direct measurement of the unobserved K0
