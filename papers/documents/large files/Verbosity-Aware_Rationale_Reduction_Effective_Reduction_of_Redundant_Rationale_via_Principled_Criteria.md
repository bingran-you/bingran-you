Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) rely on generating extensive intermediate reasoning units (e.g., tokens, sentences) to enhance final answer quality across a wide range of complex tasks. While generating multiple reasoning paths or iteratively refining rationales proves effective for improving performance, these approaches inevitably result in significantly higher inference costs. In this work, we propose a novel sentence-level rationale reduction training framework that leverages likelihoodbased crit-
eria, verbosity, to identify and remove redundant reasoning sentences. Unlike previous approaches that utilize token-level reduction, our sentence-level reduction framework maintains model performance while reducing generation length. This preserves the original reasoning abilities of LLMs and achieves an average 17.15% reduction in generation costs across various models and tasks.
1 Introduction
Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable reasoning capabilities comparable to human cognitive abilities (Madaan et al., 2024; Shinn et al., 2024; Kumar et al., 2024). These models can solve complex reasoning tasks by explicitly generating extended reasoning paths. The generation of such paths involves producing explicit reasoning units (e.g., tokens, steps) (Yu et al., 2024b), which additionally enhances model performance through iterative prompting (Wang et al.-
, 2023; Yao et al., 2023). This iterative generation of explicit reasoning paths allows the model to refine and expand its thought processes, incorporating strategic planning and ongoing cognitive generation (Xi et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2024). Although the extensive generation of explicit reasoning units leads to better peformance, it inherently leads to higher inference costs and increased
latency (Yu et al., 2024b; Wang et al., 2024). Moreover, fine-tuning LLMs using a complete reasoning path does not consistently guarantee stronger performance (Yu et al., 2024b; Deng et al., 2024b; Liu et al., 2024), suggesting the need for methods that can maintain reasoning performance while reducing the generation of reasoning units. Despite this apparent requirement, it remains underexplored how to maintain LLM reasoning capabilities while reducing intermediate reasoning paths across diverse t-
asks. Previous methods (Yu et al., 2024b) have drawn inspiration from human cognitive processes to address the aforementioned limitations. Some studies have proposed training pipelines that utilize augmented datasets, iteratively generated by foundation LLMs, to fine-tune subsequent LLMs (Yu et al., 2024b; Liu et al., 2024). However, they are inherently vulnerable as they significantly depend on the generative capabilities of LLMs. In response, other works have directly trained LLMs without augmen-
ting datasets that facilitate implicit reasoning before answer generation. Deng et al. (2023) introduced a knowledge distillation method to distill explicit reasoning into implicit reasoning through token-level hidden states. Deng et al. (2024b) selected tokens as the reasoning unit for reduction and proposed a heuristic method to internalize explicit intermediate rationale tokens. They progressively eliminate tokens from the beginning of the full rationale during the Chain of Thought (CoT) fine-t-
uning. However, these methods present a fundamental limitation as they lack sufficient justification for selecting tokens over more linguistically natural units (e.g., sentences) for reduction (Table 1), and they fail to provide principled criteria for the removal process. Furthermore, their evaluation has been predominantly conducted on synthetic arithmetic reasoning tasks, which presents limitations for real-world applications. To address these limitations, we propose a novel

3. We validate our method’s effectiveness and robustness through extensive experiments across various LLMs and ablation studies.
2 Related Works
2.1 Performance-Cost Tradeoffs in Reasoning Path Generation
Recent research has demonstrated the importance of generating iterative and refined reasoning paths to improve the reasoning capabilities of models, albeit at increased computational costs (Wang et al., 2023; Yao et al., 2023; Radha et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024; Madaan et al., 2024; Shinn et al., 2024; Kumar et al., 2024). Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2023) improves reasoning accuracy by generating more than 20 distinct reasoning paths and selecting the most consistent answer using ensemble m-
ethods, resulting in substantial computational overhead. Similarly, Tree of Thoughts (ToT) (Yao et al., 2023) expands the exploration of reasoning paths using a tree-structured method, while Strategic Chain of Thought (SCoT) (Wang et al., 2024) utilizes an Inner Dialogue Agent to adaptively explore the diverse reasoning path. Concurrently, Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2024) utilizes an iterative framework that incorporates feedback to refine responses and enhance reasoning accuracy, necessitating m-
ultiple forward passes
through the model. Additionally, the Reflexionbased framework (Shinn et al., 2024; Kumar et al., 2024) guides models to iteratively generate relevant reasoning paths through reflection for accurate answer generation. Given the necessity of iterative generation and refinement of reasoning paths to achieve optimal performance, they inherently increase inference costs and latency. Therefore, it is essential to investigate methods for efficiently generating these paths.
2.2 Reasoning Path Reduction
To address the computational costs associated with extensive reasoning paths generation, some lines of work (Yu et al., 2024b; Liu et al., 2024) have focused on generating augmented datasets with varying rationale lengths to shorten the generation of reasoning paths. Yu et al. (2024b) guide model to generate multiple reasoning paths using SelfConsistency to construct datasets, subsequently fine-tuning the model to generate direct answers without intermediate reasoning steps. Liu et al. (2024) deve-
loped a heuristic approach to merge reasoning steps and iteratively trained the model to produce shorter reasoning paths, which are subsequently incorporated into the progressive training phase. While these approaches demonstrate empirical effectiveness, they present two fundamental limitations: (1) their heavy dependence on LLM generation capabilities introduces inherent instability, and (2) their objective of reducing reasoning path necessitates the paradoxical creation of datasets requiring ext-
ensive reasoning path generation. To address these weaknesses, another line of work (Deng et al., 2023, 2024b) has focused on directly training LLM without augmented datasets. Implicit-CoT (Deng et al., 2023) utilizes a multimodel framework where an emulator model is trained to predict the teacher’s token-level hidden states, and a student model uses these predicted states to generate answers. ICoT-SI (Deng et al., 2024b) identifies tokens as reduction units, proposing a method to internalize expl-
icit intermediate rationale tokens by progressively removing them from the beginning of the reasoning path within the CoT fine-tuning process. However, these methods have shown limited generalization across diverse datasets as they have only been validated on simple arithmetic reasoning tasks, such as multiplication problems. This limitation raises concerns about their applicability to real-world scenarios where

Moreover, they do not justify their criteria for tokenlevel removal nor consider whether more linguistically natural units (e.g., sentences) might serve as more effective reduction units. Specifically, the token-level removal approach may inadvertently eliminate critical information necessary for answer generation or distort the semantic information of the sentence, while requiring repeated removal of redundant reasoning steps. Motivated by the comput-
ational costs of the extensive reasoning path generation and the limitations of existing reduction approaches, we investigate the redundancy of various sentence positions for potential removal and propose a novel method with principled criteria that can efficiently reduce reasoning paths while maintaining their effectiveness.

3.1 Quanitifying the redundancy
Before delving into the method, we first investigate which positions within the rationale sentences should be selected for reduction. When the likelihood of the answer remains unchanged after a sentence is removed from the full rationales, it indicates that the sentence may be redundant for the reasoning process. To quantify the redundancy of a sentence, we compute the negative log-likelihood
(NLL) for answer y after the sentence reduction as follows:
NLL = − log pθ(y|R′, x),
where R′ = R \ {ri}i∈S, S ⊆ I. (1)
Let R denote the complete set of rationale sentences, and I represent the full index set of these sentences. The subset of indices corresponding to sentences selected for reduction is denoted by S, and R′ represents the remaining rationale sentences after their removal. {ri}i∈S denotes the sentences selected for reduction. For simplicity, we use {ri} without the subset index notation throughout the rest of the paper.
3.2 Redundancy of leading sentences
We conduct a pilot study to empirically demonstrate the redundancy of the leading sentences within the rationales. By varying the size of {ri} from 1 to 4, we calculate the NLL of Mistral 7B (Jiang et al., 2023) across different reasoning datasets. To explore more general patterns of sentence removal, we employ a stochastic strategy in our experimental setup. We compare three configurations for sampling {ri}: front, random, and back. In the front configuration, initial sentences are assigned a hig-
her probability when forming R′, whereas random and back configurations use uniform and progressively increasing probabilities, respectively1. Additionally, we computed the NLL for full rationale sentences (i.e., − log pθ(y|R, x)) as a baseline to evaluate the impact of reduction. As depicted in Figure 1, the NLL difference between the full and reduced rationales shows a marginal likelihood difference in the front (<) configuration. 4.1 Verbosity
To quantify the redundancy of the sentences for removal, we first introduce a foundational concept ‘verbosity’. Given an input x, full rationale R, and a reduced rationale R′ = {rj}j∈I\{i}, we measure the verbosity of a sentence ri on y by measuring the difference in Kullback–Leibler divergence (KLdivergence) as follows:
verbosity(y) = DKL(q(y|x) ∥ pθ(y|R, x))

4.4 CoT Training with Rationale Reduction
In our framework, the model is trained using Equation (4) for predefined warm-up steps to inject its reasoning capabilities. After the warm-up steps, at each training step t, each sentence ri, starting from the first index sentence in R, is evaluated against either Equation (5) alone (denoted as VARR) or both Equations (5) and (9) (denoted as VARR+). Sentences satisfying these respective criteria are selected for removal and are not included in subsequent training steps. The maximum removable numb-
er of sentences at each training step t is determined based on a linear schedule, adopting Deng et al. (2024b)’s setting as follows:
r(t) = ⌊Nt · (t/T )⌋, (10)
where T represents the total number of training steps, Nt is the total number of rationale sentences at step t, and r(t) indicates the maximum number of sentences that can be removed at that step.

5.2 Baselines
We compare our method to the following baselines: Explicit-CoT (Nye et al., 2021), where the model is finetuned with explicit chain-of-thought reasoning, and ICoT-SI (Deng et al., 2024b), where the model is fine-tuned using a linear token elimination schedule. It is important to note that after removing intermediate reasoning paths, the model’s performance should be competitive with Explicit-CoT. Consequently, ICoT-SI serves as our primary baseline. We exclude Implicit-CoT (Deng et al., 2023) from-
 our baselines due to its significant memory requirements, requiring the training of three models on a single GPU. Furthermore, according to Deng et al. (2024b), this method underperformed compared to ICoT-SI. All baselines were trained
on a single A100-80GB GPU, and detailed training configurations for each method are provided in Appendix C.
5.3 Evaluation
We employ two evaluation metrics: First, we evaluate the accuracy of each method in generating the final answer for the respective tasks. Second, we count the generated tokens to evaluate reasoning efficiency while maintaining performance.
5.4 Main results
In Figure 3, we present the results for each reasoning task across different models. Our method demonstrates consistent performance improvements or maintains comparable results across most benchmarks, while also achieving more efficient reasoning path generation. Notably, we observed significant improvements in average accuracy with increments of 2.59%, 6.27%, and 14.27% for Mistral 7B, Llama3.2 1B, and Llama3.2 3B (VARR+), respectively, compared to Explicit CoT, while efficiency improved by 19.35-
%, 17.24%, and 14.88% for these models. These findings indicate that some reasoning sentences are redundant and can even disturb answer generation, highlighting that effective reasoning capabilities can be learned through selective utilization of essential reasoning units. For ICoT-SI, the model exhibited performance degradation after token removal training. Specifically, ICoT-SI shows an average performance degradation of 18.93% compared to Explicit CoT.

5.5.4 Varying the Warm-up Steps
We evaluated our method against various warmup stages on the TriviaQA and CommonsenseQA datasets. As the duration of the warm-up stages increases, the model becomes more fitted to the non-reduction dataset, which inhibits VARR’s ability to eliminate redundant sentences from the reasoning path. Consequently, as illustrated in Figure 4, longer warm-up periods result in an increase in generated tokens and a decrease in accuracy. These results suggest that 0.1 training steps provide sufficient time to-
 inject reasoning abilities while enabling the systematic reduction of redundant reasoning sentences during the learning process. Therefore, we set 0.1 as the default setting for the warm-up steps.

6 Conclusion
In this work, we propose sentence-level rationale reduction framework VARR and provide empirical evidence that models trained with non-redundant rationales achieve greater efficiency. We address the lack of principled criteria for identifying redundant sentences during the training phase by developing a reduction method that not only preserves the model’s reasoning capabilities but also reduces the likelihood of generating incorrect answers. Our experiments show that VARR can efficiently handle a -
diverse range of tasks with fewer generated tokens, without sacrificing its accuracy. Further empirical findings confirm that sentences are appropriate reasoning units for rationale reduction, highlighting the necessity of principled criteria for robust application. This work contributes novel insights to rationale reduction research, contributing to the efficient elicitation of reasoning in language models.
Limitations
While our work provides novel insights into rationale reduction research, our experiments were primarily conducted using a relatively small large language model, constrained by computational costs. Additionally, for the same reasons, it was not feasible to test the model with datasets featuring long sequences in both queries and rationales (Reddy et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2024a). However, given that the VARR/VARR+ frameworks are designed with a systematically structured approach, we believe that th-
ey could also be effective in scaled-up settings.
