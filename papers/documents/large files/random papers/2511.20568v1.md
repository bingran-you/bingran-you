On the rigidity of special and exceptional geometries with torsion a closed 3-form
Georgios Papadopoulos
Department of Mathematics King’s College London Strand London WC2R 2LS, UK
george.papadopoulos@kcl.ac.uk
Abstract
We demonstrate that all Riemannian manifolds (M, g, H) that admit a connection ∇b with torsion a 3-form H, which is both closed dH = 0 and ∇b -covariantly constant, are locally isometric to a product N × G, where G is a semisimple group and N is a Riemannian manifold with ιV H = 0 for all tangent vectors V ∈ TpN ⊂ TpM , p ∈ M . If M is simply connected and complete, then by the de Rham theorem M = N × G globally. We use this to simplify the proof of similar results for strong KT, CYT and HKT manif-
olds that obey the above hypotheses and extend them to strong G2 and Spin(7) manifolds with torsion. As an application, we describe the geometry of all complete and simply connected G2 and Spin(7) manifolds whose torsion satisfies the above conditions. We also demonstrate that all compact 8-dimensional manifolds with strong HKT structure are locally isometric to one of the following: 8-dimensional hyper-K ̈ahler; SU (3) equipped with the bi-invariant metric and 3-form; or the product (U (1) × SU (-
2)) × B4, where B4 is either a hyper-K ̈ahler manifold or U (1) × SU (2) equipped with an HKT structure.

1 Introduction
Geometries (M, g, H) with metric g equipped with a connection, ∇b , with torsion a 3-form, H, have found widespread applications in mathematics and physics, see [1]- [15] for some selected early works. This is especially the case for those that the holonomy of ∇b reduces to a proper subgroup of the orthogonal group and as consequence the underlying manifold, M , exhibits some additional structure. Such geometries include manifolds1 with a Ka ̈hler with torsion (KT), Calabi-Yau with torsion (CYT) a-
nd with hyper-K ̈ahler with torsion (HKT) structure. In such a case, the holonomy of ∇b is contained in U (k) ⊂ SO(2k), SU (k) ⊂ U (k) ⊂ SO(2k) and Sp(q) ⊂ SO(4q), respectively. Of particular interest is a subclass of these geometries that obey the strong condition – for these the torsion 3-form H is closed, dH = 0. Following the work of Hamilton [16, 17] and Perelman [18, 19] on Ricci flows and the generalisation by Oliynyk, Suneeeta, and Woolgar [20, 21] to geometries with torsion a 3-form, stro-
ng CYT and HKT appear as solitons in the context of generalised Ricci flow, see also [22, 23] for some applications. The regularity of generalised Ricci flow has been investigated by Streets and Tian. The same authors also presented applications to strong CYT manifolds in [24, 25]. For a review on generalised Ricci flow, see [26] and references therein. Recently, using generalised Ricci flows techniques, it has been demonstrated that all scale invariant sigma models with compact target space are c-
onformally invariant [27]. This was later extended to the heterotic sigma models in [28]. Strong KT and HKT structures have also appeared on the moduli space of instantons over Hermitian and HKT manifolds established by Lu ̈bke and Teleman [29] and Moraru and Verbitsky [31], respectively, see also [30] for analogous results on the geometry of the moduli spaces of instantons over generalised Hermitian or equivalently strong bi-KT manifolds. Using these results and after exploring the symmetries of -
the moduli space of instantons over the HKT manifold S1 × S3, Witten [32] provided evidence that the dual conformal theory of a certain AdS3/CFT2 correspondence is a sigma model with target space the moduli space of instantons on S1 × S3. The geometry and symmetries of moduli spaces of instantons and Hermitian-Einstein connections over manifolds with KT, bi-KT, HKT and bi-HKT structures have been also explored in [33], where it was also established that the moduli space of instantons over the HKT -
manifold S1 × S3 is locally isometric to a principal fibration over a Quaternionic manifold with torsion (QKT) [34, 35] with fibre group that has Lie algebra u(1) ⊕ su(2). Despite the widespread applications, surprisingly, very few examples of compact strong CYT and HKT manifolds with non-vanishing torsion, H ̸= 0, are known. Most of the examples constructed are (locally isometric to) products N × G, where N is a Calabi-Yau or hyper-Ka ̈hler manifold and G is a group manifold with a CYT or HKT str-
ucture, respectively. For strong CYT manifolds with a ∇b -covariantly constants torsion H, ∇b H = 0, where ∇b is the connection with torsion H a 3-form, a rigidity theorem has been demonstrated in [36, 37] confirming that all such manifolds are products N × G, where N is a Calabi-Yau manifold and G is a group manifold. For compact 6-dimensional strong CYT, a rigidity result has been demonstrated by Apostolov, Barbaro, Lee and Streets [38], where under some additional assumption such manifolds were-
 shown to be locally isometric and holomorphic to the following spaces: a Calabi-Yau manifold; S3 × S3; S3 × R × C.
In the strong HKT case, there are not known compact examples that are not locally isometric to a product manifold N × G. The authors of [39] have demonstrated that all strong HKT manifolds with ∇b -covariantly constant torsion H are locally isometric and tri-holomorphic to a

One of the objectives of this article is to generalise the above results to Riemannian manifolds that admit a connection ∇b with torsion a 3-form H such that H is both closed and ∇-
b -covariantly constant. In particular, we demonstrate the following statement.
Theorem 1.1. Let (M n, g, H) be a connected Riemannian manifold, n ≥ 3, with metric g and 3-form H. If H is closed and ∇b -covariantly constant with respect to the metric connection with torsion H, ∇b H = 0, then M is locally isometric to N × G, where G is a simply connected semisimple group with structure constants H, N is a Riemannian manifold and ιV H vanishes for any tangent vector V ∈ TpN ⊂ TpM with p ∈ M . Moreover, if M is also simply connected and complete, then M = N × G globally.
The results of the above theorem can be adapted to strong G2 and Spin(7) manifolds, (M 7, g, H, φ) and (M 8, g, H, φ), respectively, with torsion a ∇b -covariantly constant 3-form H. These can be seen as special cases of the theorem 1.1. The geometries of such manifolds are described in theorems 3.1 and 3.2, respectively. It is found that in all cases, such manifolds are locally isometric to either a group manifold or to a product of a hyper-K ̈ahler manifold with a group manifold. We provide a li-
st of all cases that can occur. We also give the classification of all complete, simply connected strong G2 and Spin(7) manifolds that satisfy the hypotheses of theorem 1.1, see also the remarks that accompany the theorems 3.1 and 3.2 below. The result in theorem 1.1 can be used to simplify similar statements that have already been made in the literature for strong KT, CYT and HKT manifolds, [36, 37, 39]. The proof for strong KT, strong CYT and strong HKT manifolds has the additional difficulty of-
 demonstrating the splitting of the complex structure. The results are described in the theorems 2.3 and 2.4 for completeness. The outcome of all these results is that requiring H to be ∇b -covariantly constant is rather restrictive to give examples of KT, CYT and HKT manifolds that are not products of group manifolds with other spaces. We investigate the question on whether ∇b -covariantly constant requirement can be replaced with a weaker condition, like for example that the (pointwise) length, -
|H|, of H is constant. Under such an assumption and for (M, g, H) compact, steady, generalised Ricci soliton, an application of the formula stated in proposition 3.1 and proven in [27], implies that H is harmonic. We explore this and point out that for a positive definite Riemann curvature, H is ∇-covariantly constant. This and some additional properties are described in proposition 3.2. It has been known for sometime that compact conformally balanced strong HKT (and CYT) are hyper-K ̈ahler (Calab-

2.2 KT and CYT manifolds with parallel 3-form torsion
The main purpose of this section is to adapt the results of the main theorem 1.1 to strong KT and CYT manifolds. These results have been obtained before in [36, 37] but they are included here as their proof is simplified in view of theorem 1.1 and for completeness. For a recent concise review of the properties of KT, CYT and HKT geometries, see [27].

Proof. The compact simply connected semisimple groups G up to dimensional 6 are isomorphic to either SU (2) or to SU (2) × SU (2).
