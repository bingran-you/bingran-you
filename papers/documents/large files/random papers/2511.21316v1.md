Abstract
It is well known that the Minkowski dimension of spiral trajectories near a non-degenerate focus in analytic (smooth) systems is in one-to-one correspondence with the cyclicity of the focus in generic unfoldings. We give a complete fractal treatment, in terms of the Minkowski dimension and (non-)degeneracy, of spiral trajectories near pseudo foci of piecewise analytic systems. We hope these results will prove useful in the study of bifurcations of such pseudo foci through inherent geometry of asso-
ciated spiral orbits.

1.2 Pseudo foci in piecewise analytic systems
In this paper we consider the following model for piecewise analytic systems. Let V ± = M ±∂x + N ±∂y be analytic vector fields in an open neighbourhood of the origin in R2. If N +(x, 0)N −(x, 0) > 0 for x ̸= 0, one naturally defines the flow of the piecewise analytic vector field V = 1{y≥0}V + + 1{y≤0}V − to agree with the flow of V + in the upper half plane {y > 0}, with the flow of V − in the lower half plane {y < 0}, and to switch between them as it crosses the x−axis. Depending of the local b-
ehaviour of V + and V − at the origin, this can result in the origin being a focus-like fixed point of the flow of V . We study the following three types of such pseudo foci, as defined in [CGP01]. For the sake of simplicity, we will assume that the resulting flow follows a counter-clockwise direction around the origin, which is equivalent to sgn N +(x, 0) = sgn N −(x, 0) = sgn x.

2 First return maps
For a complete understanding of pseudo foci, one would like to characterize maps that can be realized as first return maps of such fixed points. Unfortunately, we are not able to obtain a result of such strength, but it is not hard to obtain a weaker version of the results that will be sufficient for our further fractal considerations. Let’s first recall the structure of the first return maps associated to non-degenerate foci in analytic systems.
2.1 First return maps of non-degenerate foci
Let the origin be a non-degenerate monodromic singularity (associated eigenvalues have non-zero imaginary part) of an analytic vector field, and let γ : ⟨−ε′, ε′⟩ → R2, ε′ > 0, be an analytic parametrization of an arbitrary line segment through the origin, such that γ(0) = (0, 0). Then there is an ε ∈ ⟨0, ε′⟩,

and an analytic map h : ⟨−ε, ε⟩ → ⟨−ε′, ε′⟩, satisfying h′(0) < 0, such that γ(h(t)) is the first point where a forward trajectory through γ(t) intersects the image of γ. Moreover, P := h ◦ h represents the first return to γ (⟨0, ε′⟩) and to γ (⟨−ε′, 0⟩). A standard result (see for instance [Rou98]) is that either P (t) ≡ t, or that the associated displacement map is of the form
∆(t) := P (t) − t = α2k+1t2k+1 + o(t2k+1),

2.2 Semi-monodromy maps of non-degenerate foci
In the study of foci in analytic systems, one is usually interested in the monodromy map. However, since in piecewise smooth systems we study, the transitions through the upper and lower half-plane define different ”semi-monodromy” maps, we will restrict our attention to these half-turns. We have seen in subsection 2.1 that the semi-monodromy associated to a non-degenerate monodromic singularity is analytic in any analytic parametrization of the transversal. A natural question to pose is ”what are-
 all the analytic germs that can be realized as such semi-mondromy maps?”. Unfortunately, we don’t have an answer to this question, but we obtain the following weaker version of the result. It will be sufficient for our considerations.
Proposition 1. Let h(t) = −λt+o(t), λ > 0, be a real analytic germ at 0. Then there is an analytic vector field with a non-degenerate monodromic singularity at the origin, such that the associated semi-monodromy map m is a representative of the formal class of h. Moreover, for an arbitrary N ∈ N, such a vector field can be chosen so that h(t) − m(t) = o(tN ).
Remark 2. Notice that the realization of h is equivalent to the realization of any representative m of its analytic class, that is any m such that there is a local diffeomorphism φ, analytic at 0, satisfying h = φ ◦ m ◦ φ−1. Indeed, if m is the semi-monodromy of some analytic vector field V , then φ ◦ m ◦ φ−1 is the semi-monodromy of the vector field Φ∗V , where Φ(x, y) := (φ(x), y).
Since the determination of an analytic class is a hard problem, we work with formal classes of maps.

5 Conclusions and perspectives
This manuscript provides a complete fractal treatment of pseudo-foci of types introduced in [CGP01]. Notice that all our calculations only depend on the first non-zero term in the asymptotic expansion of the displacement maps associated to the first return map, so similar results are expected to hold for C∞ systems, with possible small alterations of some arguments. During consultations with the author’s thesis defense committee, Prof. Pavao Mardeˇsi ́c suggested generalizations of these results i-
n the spirit of the work of Nakai [Nak94]. The first return maps we have studied are of the form P = h−◦h+, where h± are the semimonodromy maps in the upper and lower half plane respectively, and an orbit of a point can be understood as the action of a pseudogroup G = {P n : n ∈ N0} on a point. The work of Nakai involves the study of generalized orbits under the action of general nonsolvable pseudogroups of holomorphic diffeomorphisms in (C, 0). Motivated by this one can consider orbits under the -
action of the pseudogroup generated by S = {h− ◦h+, h+ ◦h−, h+ ◦h+, h− ◦h−}. These orbits can be thought of as sets of points of intersection of a transversal and a trajectory of
23

a piecewise analytic system where the switch between the two subsystems does not necessarily happen every time we intersect the x-axis. This approach can even be enriched with a stochastic element, where one might study the expected or a.s. fractal properties of such orbits, under certain probability distributions that determine the switch between the two regimes.
