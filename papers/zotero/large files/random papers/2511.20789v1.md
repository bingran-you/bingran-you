GRADED CONTACT GEOMETRY AND THE AKSZ FORMALISM
IVAN CONTRERAS, NICOLAS MARTINEZ ALBA, AND RAJAN AMIT MEHTA
Abstract. The AKSZ formalism is a construction of topological field theories where the target spaces are differential graded symplectic manifolds. In this paper, we describe an analogue of the AKSZ formalism where the target spaces are differential graded contact manifolds. We show that the space of fields inherits a weak contact structure, and we construct a solution to the analogue of the classical master equation, defined via the Jacobi bracket. In the n = 1 case, we recover the Jacobi sigma mo-
del, and in the n = 2 case, we obtain three-dimensional topological field theories associated to Courant-Jacobi algebroids.
1. Introduction
Topological Field Theories have been studied extensively over the past decades. In particular, the formulation using supergeometry, known as the AKSZ formalism [1] has been fundamental in the study of field theories with boundary [6], deformation quantization [19], the Poisson sigma model [5], among others. The AKSZ method constructs solutions of the BV master equation directly, without the need of starting from a classical action with a set of symmetries, in comparison to the BV formalism. The cl-
assical action can be recovered by setting the nonzero degree fields to zero. In the AKSZ formalism, the target space is assumed to be a differential graded symplectic manifold. In low degrees, the construction leads to the Poisson sigma model and the Courant sigma model, a special case of which is Chern-Simons theory. In this manuscript, we describe an analogue of the AKSZ formalism where the target space is a differential graded contact manifold. We prove results that are analogous to those in o-
rdinary AKSZ theory, namely that the space of fields inherits a graded contact structure, and

2. Overview of contact geometry
In this section, we give a brief overview of contact geometry, covering notation and basic results that will be used later in the paper. For more details, see [15, 23].
2.1. Contact structures and contact forms. Let M be a finitedimensional manifold, and let D ⊆ T M be a corank 1 distribution on M . Locally, D can be expressed as the kernel of a 1-form α. If such a 1form is chosen, then the 2-form dα induces a map (dα)♭ : T M → T ∗M , X 7→ iXdα.

If a contact form for a contact structure D exists globally, then D is said to be coorientable. Throughout this paper, we will assume coorientability unless stated otherwise. If α is a contact form and f is any nonvanishing smooth function, then α′ = f α is another contact form for the same contact structure. It should be stressed that the isomorphisms in parts (2) and (3) of Proposition 2.2 depend on the choice of contact form.
2.2. Contact vector fields. Let M be a manifold with contact structure D. A vector field X ∈ X(M ) is called a contact vector field if it preserves the contact structure, i.e. if [X, Y ] ∈ Γ(D) for all Y ∈ Γ(D). If α is a contact form, then we can equivalently characterize contact vector fields by the property that
LXα = gα
for some g ∈ C∞(M ). An important example of a contact vector field is the Reeb vector field R, which is defined by the equation ♭(R) = α. Equivalently, R is the unique solution to the equations
iRα = 1, iRdα = 0.
We emphasize that the Reeb vector field depends on the choice of contact form α. The following lemma provides a bijection between smooth functions and contact vector fields.

3. Graded contact geometry
This section contains a brief review of graded manifolds and graded contact structures. For more details on graded manifolds, see [7,14,24]. Graded contact structures are described in [16, 25], and the closely related theory of graded symplectic structures is in [27].
3.1. Graded manifolds. A graded manifold M is a manifold M equipped with a sheaf OM of graded-commutative Z-graded algebras such that, locally,
(3.1) OM(U ) ∼= C∞(U ) ⊗ S(W ),
where W is a Z-graded vector space. It is usually assumed that W does not have a degree 0 component. We think of OM as being the sheaf of “smooth functions” on M, and we will use C(M) to denote the global sections of OM. The local form (3.1) implies that there exist graded local coordinates, where the degree 0 coordinates are coordinates on M , the functions are smooth in the degree 0 coordinates and polynomial in the nonzero degree coordinates, and odd degree functions anti–commute with each othe-
r. We will use the notation |f | to denote the degree of a homogeneous function. We will often assume that the sheaf OM is nonnegatively graded. In this case, M is said to be an N-manifold. The following basic example will often appear in this paper.
Example 3.1. (Shifted vector bundles). If E → M is a vector bundle, then the N-manifold E[1] is given by OE[1] = ∧Γ(E∗). In particular, the algebra of functions on T [1]M is the algebra Ω(M ) of differential forms on M , and the algebra of functions on T ∗[1]M is the algebra X•(M ) = ∧Γ(T M ) of multivector fields on M .
Many differential geometric constructions on manifolds can be defined more generally on graded manifolds. In particular, a (degree k) vector field on M is defined as a (degree k) graded derivation of OM. The space X(M) of vector fields on M, endowed with the graded commutator bracket
[X, Y ] = XY − (−1)|X||Y |Y X,
is a graded Lie algebra.
Example 3.2 (Euler vector field). On any graded manifold, there is a natural degree 0 vector field called the Euler vector field ε, given by ε(f ) = |f |f for any homogeneous function f .

5. The Jacobi sigma model
In this section, we consider the n = 1 case of AKSZ-contact theory and show that it reproduces the Jacobi sigma model of [2].
5.1. Jacobi manifolds. Let M be a manifold. A Jacobi structure on M is a Lie bracket on C∞(M ) that is local, in the sense that the support of {f, g} is contained in the supports of both f and g. A theorem due to Kirillov [18] says that local Lie brackets are always first-order in each entry. From this it can be deduced that a Jacobi structure is
