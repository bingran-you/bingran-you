My Current Research 

Currently, I am focusing on advancing the scalability and reliability of trapped ion quantum computing systems by leveraging integrated photonics technologies. I am actively leading and contributing to two major projects that are developing next-generation photonic integrated circuits (PICs) to address individual trapped ion qubits with high fidelity and low crosstalk—one of the key technical bottlenecks for realizing large-scale quantum processors.

The first project, supported by the UC Noyce Initiative, explores adjoint-optimized multimode photonic circuits for scalable trapped ion addressing. In this work, we design and fabricate lithographically defined nanophotonic waveguide devices integrated directly with surface-electrode ion traps. Our platform demonstrates diffraction-limited beam focusing to trapped 40Ca+ ions via engineered focusing grating couplers, with experimentally validated crosstalk suppression down to −40 dB using multimod-
e waveguide strategies. This technology enables precise, stable, and compact light delivery for individual qubit control, and it represents a transformative step toward fully integrated and modular quantum processors. A manuscript describing this work is currently under review at npj Nanophotonics.

The second project, funded by the NSF Challenge Institute for Quantum Computation, develops a novel 2D-3D photonic integration platform that achieves broadband, achromatic, and high-efficiency optical addressing for tightly spaced ion arrays. By combining planar waveguide lenses with 3D-printed biconic mirrors, we achieve record-setting 30 dB crosstalk performance at ion pitches as small as 5 µm for key gate wavelengths (532 nm and 729 nm). Additionally, our monolithic surface-trap integration is -
designed to mitigate photoinduced dielectric charging—a key issue in practical ion trap operation. These innovations will significantly enhance the control fidelity and device density for future quantum processors and sensors.

Both projects aim to lay the foundation for building modular, chip-integrated trapped ion systems, which are critical for the U.S. quantum computing roadmap. These efforts directly align with national interests in advancing quantum technologies for secure communication, computational supremacy, and precision metrology, and are supported by U.S. public funding.
