SYLOW SUBGROUPS AND THE NUMBER OF IRREDUCIBLE CHARACTERS OF DEGREES DIVISIBLE BY A PRIME p
JAMES P. COSSEY, MARK L. LEWIS, A. A. SCHAEFFER FRY, AND HUNG P. TONG-VIET
Abstract. Let G be a finite group and p a prime. We establish an upper bound for the derived length of a Sylow p-subgroup of G in terms of the number of irreducible characters of G whose degrees are divisible by p. We also prove that if B is a p-block of a finite p-solvable group G with defect group D, then the derived length of D is at most one more than the number of ordinary irreducible characters of positive height in B.
1. Introduction
Let G be a finite group and p a prime. The Itˆo–Michler theorem asserts that p divides the degrees of no complex irreducible characters of G if and only if G possesses a normal abelian Sylow p-subgroup. Fix a Sylow p-subgroup P of G, and let np(G) denote the number of complex irreducible characters of G whose degrees are divisible by p. We refer to such characters as psingular characters, and their degrees as p-singular character degrees. We write Irr(G) for the set of complex irreducible characte-
rs of G, and cd(G) for the set of their degrees. From the Itˆo–Michler theorem, if np(G) = 0, then the derived length of P , denoted dl(P ), equals 1. In particular, in this case we have dl(P ) = np(G) + 1. The finite groups satisfying np(G) = 1 were completely classified by Guralnick et al. [16]. For such a group, we have |cdp(G)| = 1, where cdp(G) denotes the set of p-singular character degrees of G. Isaacs, Moret ́o, Navarro, and Tiep proved in [20] that, for such groups G, the Sylow psubgroup -
P is metabelian; that is, dl(P ) ≤ 2 = |cdp(G)| + 1. In particular, they conjectured that |cd(P )| ≤ |cdp(G)| + 1. Since P is an M -group, Taketa’s theorem ([18, Corollary 5.13]) yields dl(P ) ≤ |cd(P )|. Moreover, it is clear that |cdp(G)| ≤ np(G). Thus the above conjecture would imply that dl(P ) ≤ |cd(P )| ≤ |cdp(G)| + 1 ≤ np(G) + 1.
Therefore, if the above conjecture holds, then dl(P ) ≤ np(G) + 1. In [10], the authors verify Isaacs-Moret ́o-Navarro-Tiep’s conjecture for symmetric groups, finite general linear or unitary groups. Navarro also posed the following question in [37]: Is it true that dl(P ) ≤ |cdp(G)| + 1? In this paper, we prove the above weaker inequality.
Theorem 1. Let G be a finite group and let p be a prime. Let P be a Sylow p-subgroup of G. Then the derived length of P is at most one more than the number of of irreducible characters of G whose degrees are divisible by p; that is, dl(P ) ≤ np(G) + 1.
We recall the Isaacs–Seitz conjecture ([18, p. 206]), which states that if G is a finite solvable group, then dl(G) ≤ |cd(G)|. To prove Theorem 1, we first establish it for finite p-solvable groups and then reduce the general case to finite nonabelian simple groups. In particular, the following result is needed to complete the proof. For a finite group G and a prime p, let n∗p(G) denote the number of Aut(G)-orbits on the set of all p-singular irreducible characters of G. Note that
|cdp(G)| ≤ n∗
p(G) ≤ np(G).
Theorem 2. Let G be a finite nonabelian simple group and p a prime dividing |G|. Then, for any Sylow p-subgroup P of G, dl(P ) ≤ n∗p(G).

For sporadic simple groups, one can show that dl(P ) ≤ |cdp(G)|, with equality occurring only in the case (G, p) = (M22, 2). For alternating groups An with n ≥ 5 and for simple groups of Lie type, we prove that dl(P ) ≤ n∗p(G). These results are established in Section 4. We now turn to the theory of Brauer blocks. Let p be a prime and let B be a Brauer p-block of G with defect group D = D(B). Write |P | = pa and |D| = pd, so that d is called the defect of the block B. Denote by Irr(B) the set of o-
rdinary irreducible characters belonging to B. For each χ ∈ Irr(B), the height of χ, denoted ht(χ), is defined by the relation
χ(1)p = p a−d+ht(χ),
where, for a positive integer n, np denotes the largest power of p dividing n. Let np(B) denote the number of characters χ ∈ Irr(B) of positive height. The Brauer height zero conjecture, recently settled in [30], asserts that for any p-block B of G, the defect group D is abelian if and only if every character in Irr(B) has height zero. Equivalently, np(B) = 0 if and only if dl(D) = 1. In particular, one always has dl(D) ≤ np(B)+1 in this case. This inequality holds trivially when B has p-defect ze-
ro (since D = 1). When D has full defect, i.e. d = a, the quantity np(B) counts the number of characters in Irr(B) whose degrees are divisible by p. We therefore propose the following conjecture.
Conjecture 3. Let G be a finite group and p a prime. Let B be a Brauer p-block of G with defect group D. Then there exists an absolute constant k > 0, independent of B, p and D, such that dl(D) ≤ np(B) + k.
In our next result, we show that one may take k = 1 when G is p-solvable.
Theorem 4. Let G be a finite p-solvable group, and let B be a p-block of G with defect group D. Then dl(D) ≤ np(B) + 1.
There is a related conjecture due to Navarro stating that dl(D) ≤ |cd(B)|, where cd(B) = {χ(1) : χ ∈ Irr(B)}. See [13] for several results concerning this conjecture. However, it is not always the case that |cd(B)| ≤ np(B) + 1 or vice versa.
Example. Let G = S9 be the symmetric group of degree 9 and let p = 2. Then G has two p-blocks, say B0 = B0(G), the principal block with defect group P , where P is a Sylow 2subgroup of G, and the block B1 with defect 4. For the principal block, we have |cd(B0)| = 10 and n2(B0) + 1 = 13. For B1, we have |cd(B1)| = 5 and n2(B1) + 1 = 3.
We denote by ht(B) the set of all character heights in Irr(B). It is clear that | ht(B)| ≤ |cd(B)| and | ht(B)| ≤ np(B)+1. In [13], it was shown that if G ∈ {An, Sn, GLn(q)}, where q is a power of p, then dl(D) ≤ | ht(B)| for every block B of G with defect group D. In particular, Conjecture 3 holds in these cases with k = 1. It is also true in the case when G is a finite p-group. Note, however, that the inequality dl(D) ≤ | ht(B)| does not hold in general; counterexamples were constructed in [13].-
 A second related conjecture is the Eaton-Moret ́o conjecture from [8]. For a finite p-group P define m(P ) to be the minimal nonlinear degree of irreducible characters in Irr(P ) and set mh(P ) by m(P ) = pmh(P ). Define mh(B) to be the minimum among positive numbers in ht(B). (Both mh(P ) and mh(B) are defined to be ∞ if no such degree, resp. height, exists.) The EatonMoret ́o conjecture suggests for all groups G that a block B with defect group D should satisfy mh(B) = mh(D). This conjecture ha-
s been proved for p-solvable groups by Navarro in [38] and for general linear and unitary groups by Feng, Liu, and Zhang in [9]. In general, this conjecture is still open, although an important case was completed in [29] and minimal counterexamples were studied in [31].

In Section 3, we prove Theorem 1 for p-solvable groups, and then we show how to prove Theorem 1 provided Theorem 2 is true. Theorem 2 is proved in Section 4. 3. Solvable and p-solvable groups
In this section, we prove the main theorem for finite p-solvable groups and reduce the proof of the main theorem to proving Theorem 2. Recall that for a finite group G and a prime p, np(G) is the number of irreducible characters of G that are divisible by p.
Theorem 3.1. Let p be a prime, let G be a finite p-solvable group, and let P be a Sylow p-subgroup of G. Then dl(P ) ≤ np(G) + 1.
Proof. We work by induction on |G|. Fix n = np(G). If n = 0, then by the Itˆo-Michler theorem, we know that P is abelian (and normal in G), and so, the result holds. Thus, we may assume that n ≥ 1. Note that this implies that p divides |G| and that G is not abelian. Let N be a minimal normal subgroup of G and we have that 1 < N < G. Notice that either N is an elementary abelian p-group or p does not divide |N |. We first suppose that np(G/N ) < n. Let m = np(G/N ), and note that m ≤ n − 1. By the -
inductive hypothesis applied to G/N , we have that dl(P N/N ) ≤ m + 1. Thus, P (m+1) ≤ N . Since P ∩N is abelian, this implies that P (m+2) = 1, and so, P (n+1) = 1. We have dl(P ) ≤ n+1. Next, suppose that p does not divide |N |. This implies that N ∩ P = 1. By the inductive hypothesis applied to G/N , we have that dl(P N/N ) ≤ n + 1. Thus, P (n+1) ≤ N . Since P ∩ N = 1, we have P (n+1) = 1, and thus, dl(P ) ≤ n + 1.

We are left with the case that np(G/N ) = n and p divides |N |. Since G is p-solvable, this implies that N is a p-group, and so, N ≤ P . We claim that N ∩ P ′ = 1. Suppose that N ∩ P ′ > 1. Then there exists a character 1 ̸= μ ∈ Irr(N ∩ P ′). Let ν ∈ Irr(N | μ), and suppose θ ∈ Irr(P | ν). Since μ is a constituent of θN∩P ′ and μ ̸= 1, we deduce that P ′ is not contained in the kernel of θ. Hence, p divides θ(1). Suppose χ ∈ Irr(G | θ) and observe that θ is a constituent of χP , and so, ν is a con-
stituent of χN and μ is a constituent of χN∩P ′. Let γ be any irreducible constituent of χP and let δ be an irreducible constituent of γN . We know that δ is conjugate to ν in G since N is normal in G and δ and ν are both constituents of χN . We may write δ = νg for some g ∈ G. As N is abelian, ν is an extension of μ. Thus, δ is an extension of μg. Now, μg is nontrivial, so P ′ is not contained in the kernel of γ. This implies that p divides γ(1). Since γ was arbitrary, we see that p divides the d-
egrees of all of the irreducible constituents of χP , and this implies that p divides χ(1). This is a contradiction since all of the irreducible characters of G whose degrees are divisible by p have N in their kernels. Thus, the claim is proved and N ∩ P ′ = 1. By the inductive hypothesis applied to G/N , we have dl(P N/N ) ≤ n + 1. Thus, P (n+1) ≤ N. Since P ′ ∩ N = 1, we have P (n+1) = 1, and thus, dl(P ) ≤ n + 1. This proves the theorem. □
In fact, we can generalize this further. Fix a prime p. We say that a finite group G is a Cp-group if all of the nonsolvable composition factors satisfy the hypothesis that the number of orbits of the irreducible characters whose degree is a multiple of p under their automorphism group is an upper bound for the derived length of their Sylow p-subgroups.
Theorem 3.2. Let p be a prime, let G be a Cp-group, and let P be a Sylow p-subgroup of G. Then dl(P ) ≤ np(G) + 1.
Proof. We work by induction on |G|. By Theorem 3.1, we may assume that G is not p-solvable. Fix n = np(G). If G is nonabelian simple, then since we are assuming that G is a Cp-group, we have a stronger result than the desired conclusion, so we are good. Also, if n = 0, then by the Itˆo-Michler theorem, we know that P is abelian (and normal in G), and so, the result holds. Thus, we may assume that n ≥ 1. Note that this implies that p divides |G| and that G is not abelian. Let N be a minimal normal -
subgroup of G and we have that 1 < N < G. We first suppose that np(G/N ) < n. Let m = np(G/N ), and note that m ≤ n − 1. By the inductive hypothesis applied to G/N , we have that dl(P N/N ) ≤ m + 1. Thus, P (m+1) ≤ N . If N is abelian, then dl(P ) ≤ m + 2 ≤ n + 1. Thus, we may assume that N is nonabelian. We know that N is a direct product of copies of some nonabelian finite simple group S. In particular, N = Sk for some positive integer k. Let Q be a Sylow p-subgroup of S, and we know that a Sylo-
w subgroup of N is isomorphic to Qk. Notice that we have at most n − m orbits of irreducible characters of N whose degrees are divisible by p. This yields at most n − m orbits of irreducible characters of S whose degrees are divisible by p. Since we are assuming that G is a Cp-group, this implies that dl(Q) ≤ n − m. Since Q and Qk have the same derived length, we obtain dl(Qk) ≤ n − m. This yields (P (m+1))(n−m) = P (n−m+m+1) = P (n+1) = 1. We deduce that dl(P ) ≤ n + 1. Next, suppose that p does -
not divide |N |. This implies that N ∩ P = 1. By the inductive hypothesis applied to G/N , we have that dl(P N/N ) ≤ n + 1. Thus, P (n+1) ≤ N . Since P ∩ N = 1, we have P (n+1) = 1, and thus, dl(P ) ≤ n + 1. We are left with the case np(G/N ) = n and p divides |N |. This implies that there are no irreducible characters of N whose degree is divisible by p. By the Itˆo-Michler Theorem, this implies that N has a normal abelian Sylow p-subgroup. Since N is either elementary abelian or the direct produ-
ct of copies of a nonabelian simple group, we conclude that N must be an elementary abelian p-group. Let 1N ̸= ν ∈ Irr(N ), and let χ ∈ Irr(G | ν). We see that p does not divide χ(1); so if T is the stabilizer of ν in G, then p does not divide |G : T |. Hence, T contains a Sylow p-subgroup P of G. Now, we know that p does not divide γ(1)/ν(1) for all γ ∈ Irr(T | ν). By the Navarro-Tiep theorem ([39, Theorem A]), this implies that P/N is abelian. Hence, dl(P ) ≤ 2 which meets the bound since n ≥ 1.-

4. Finite simple groups
The aim of this section is to prove Theorem 2. That is, we show that every finite nonabelian simple group is a Cp-group, as defined before Theorem 3.2. Let G be a finite group and let p be a prime. Denote by Irrp(G) the set of irreducible characters of G whose degrees are divisible by p. Define
cdp(G) = {χ(1) : χ ∈ Irrp(G)}
as the set of degrees of such characters. For brevity, we call characters in Irrp(G) p-singular characters. As before, let np(G) = | Irrp(G)|, and let n∗p(G) denote the number of orbits in Irrp(G) under the action of the automorphism group Aut(G) of G. Since all characters within the same Aut(G)-orbit have the same degrees, it is clear that
|cdp(G)| ≤ n∗
p(G).
We follow the notation and terminology of [35] for block theory. Suppose p | |G|, and let B be a Brauer p-block of G with defect group D = D(B). Write |G|p = pa and |D| = pd, so that the defect of the block B is the nonnegative integer d = d(B). For each χ ∈ Irr(B), the degree χ(1) satisfies
χ(1)p = pa−d+ht(χ),
where ht(χ) denotes the height of χ (see [35, Corollary 3.17]). Note that if B is a block of G with defect d < a, then pa−d divides χ(1) for all χ ∈ Irr(B). In particular, each such degree is divisible by p.
