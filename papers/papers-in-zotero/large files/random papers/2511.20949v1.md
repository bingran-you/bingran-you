A RIGIDITY THEOREM FOR COMPLEX KLEINIAN GROUPS
RICHARD CANARY, TENGREN ZHANG, AND ANDREW ZIMMER
Abstract. Farre, Pozzetti and Viaggi proved that any (d−k)-hyperconvex subgroup of PSL(d, C) is virtually isomorphic to a convex cocompact Kleinian group and that its αk-critical exponent is at most 2. We show that a (d − k)-hyperconvex subgroup is isomorphic to a uniform lattice in PSL(2, C) if and only if its αk-critical exponent is exactly 2. Furthermore, we show that if a strongly irreducible (d − k)-hyperconvex subgroup has αk-critical exponent 2, then it is the image of a uniform lattice in -
PSL(2, C) by an irreducible representation of PSL(2, C) into PSL(d, C).

1. Introduction
In this paper, we continue the study of (d − k)-hyperconvex subgroups of PSL(d, C) initiated by Pozzetti–Sambarino–Wienhard [30] and Farre–Pozzetti–Viaggi [19]. A subgroup of PSL(d, C) is (d − k)-hyperconvex for some k ∈ {1, . . . , d − 1} if it is Anosov with respect to the simple roots αk−1, αk and αk+1 and triples of points in its limit set satisfy a specific transversality condition (see Section 2.5 for the definition). The most basic examples of k-hyperconvex groups are obtained by considerin-
g the image of a convex cocompact subgroup of PSL(2, C) by an irreducible representation of PSL(2, C) into PSL(d, C). Farre–Pozzetti–Viaggi showed that any (d−k)-hyperconvex subgroup of PSL(d, C) is virtually isomorphic to a convex cocompact subgroup of PSL(2, C). They further showed that the αkcritical exponent δαk (Γ) of a k-hyperconvex subgroup Γ of PSL(d, C) is at most 2. Recall that the αk-critical exponent δαk (Γ) is the exponential growth rate of the number of elements γ ∈ Γ so that
αk(κ(γ)) = log σk(γ)
σk+1(γ) ≤ T,
where σ1(γ) ≥ · · · ≥ σd(γ) are the singular values of γ.

It then follows from work of Pozzetti–Sambarino–Wienhard [30], that if Γ is (d−k)-hyperconvex, then the Hausdorff dimension of the limit set Λk(Γ) of the action of Γ on the Grassmanian Grk(Cd) is exactly δαk (Γ) ≤ 2. If Γ is virtually isomorphic to a lattice in PSL(2, C), then Λk(Γ) is homeomorphic to a two-sphere, so we must have δαk (Γ) = 2. In this paper, we prove that the converse also holds.
Theorem 1.1. If Γ ⊂ PSL(d, C) is (d − k)-hyperconvex, then δαk (Γ) = 2 if and only if Γ is virtually isomorphic to a uniform lattice in PSL(2, C).
One may view this as a generalization of the fact, due to Sullivan [34] and Tukia [36], that the critical exponent of a convex cocompact subgroup of PSL(2, C) has Hausdorff dimension two if and only if it is a uniform lattice.
The only known examples where Γ is (d − k)-hyperconvex, strongly irreducible and virtually isomorphic to a lattice in PSL(2, C) are obtained from lattices in PSL(2, C) by applying an irreducible representation of PSL(2, C) into PSL(d, C). We show that these are the only examples.
Theorem 1.2. If Γ ⊂ PSL(d, C) is (d − k)-hyperconvex, strongly irreducible and δαk (Γ) = 2, then there exist a uniform lattice Γ′ ⊂ PSL(2, C) and an irreducible representation τ : PSL(2, C) → PSL(d, C) so that Γ = τ (Γ′).
Since the property of being (d−k)-hyperconvex is stable (see [30, Prop. 6.2]), one may view our result as a global analogue of the local rigidity result of Raghunathan [31] (see also Menal–Ferrer and Porti [26, 29]) who proved that if Γ is a uniform lattice in PSL(2, C), then τ |Γ is locally rigid in the character variety X(Γ, PSL(d, C)) if τ : PSL(2, C) → PSL(d, C) is an irreducible representation. As a corollary to Theorem 1.2 we have the following.
Corollary 1.3. If Γ′ ⊂ PSL(2, C) is a uniform lattice and ρ : Γ′ → PSL(d, C) is a strongly irreducible representation with finite kernel and (d − k)-hyperconvex image, then ρ extends to an irreducible representation PSL(2, C) → PSL(d, C).
Any uniform lattice in PSL(2, C) is strongly irreducible and 1-hyperconvex, hence Corollary 1.3 can be viewed as a generalization of Mostow’s rigidity theorem [28] for hyperbolic 3-manifolds.
We also note that without irreducibility, there is considerable less rigidity. For instance, fix a uniform lattice Γ0 ⊂ SL(2, C) and any representation ρ : Γ0 → SL(d0, C). Next let τd : SL(2, C) → SL(d, C) denote the standard irreducible representation and define ρd : Γ0 → PSL(d + d0, C) by

The context. We would like to place our results into two natural contexts. One may naturally ask what restrictions on an Anosov group place restrictions on its isomorphism type. One early example of a result of this form is due to Guichard [21] who showed that if Γ is a P1-Anosov subgroup of PSL(d, R) which is isomorphic to a surface group, so that any d lines in Λ1(Γ) span Rd, then Γ is the image of a Hitchin representation. 2.5. Hyperconvex groups. With θk ⊂ ∆ as in the previous subsection, a subgroup Γ ⊂ PSL(d, K) is (d − k)-hyperconvex if it is Pθk -Anosov, and for all mutually distinct triples x, y, z ∈ Λθ(Γ) we have
(xk ∩ zd−k+1) + zd−k−1 ∩ (yk ∩ zd−k+1) + zd−k−1 = zd−k−1.
Notice that Γ is (d − 1)-hyperconvex if and only if for all mutually distinct triples x, y, z ∈ Λθ1(Γ), we have
x1 + y1 + zd−2 = Kd.
This condition was called (1, 1, 2)-hyperconvexity by Pozzetti–Sambarino–Wienhard [30]. They showed that in this setting the α1-Patterson–Sullivan measure behaves much like a conformal measure in the rank one setting. They also used this observation to bound the α1-critical exponent.
Theorem 2.3 ([30, Cor. 6.9 and 7.3]). If Γ ⊂ PSL(d, C) is (d−1)-hyperconvex, then the Hausdorff dimension of Λ1(Γ) is equal to δα1(Γ). Moreover, δα1(Γ) ≤ 2.
Farre–Pozzetti–Viaggi [19] showed that hyperconvex groups are always virtually isomorphic to convex cocompact Kleinian groups. We recall that two groups G and H are virtually isomorphic if there exists finite index subgroups G′ ⊂ G and H′ ⊂ H and finite normal subgroups M ⊂ G′ and N ⊂ H′ so that G′/M is isomorphic to H′/N .
Theorem 2.4 ([19, Thm. A]). If Γ ⊂ PSL(d, C) is k-hyperconvex for some k ∈ {1, . . . , d − 1}, then Γ is virtually isomorphic to a convex cocompact subgroup of SL(2, C).
They further adapt Ahlfors’ proof [2] that a convex cocompact Kleinian group is not cocompact, then its limit set has measure zero, to control the 2-dimensional Hausdorff measure of limit sets of hyperconvex groups.
Theorem 2.5 ([19, Thm. B]). If Γ ⊂ PSL(d, C) is (d−k)-hyperconvex and ∂Γ is not a two-sphere, then the 2-dimensional Hausdorff measure of Λk(Γ) is zero.
