Two-photon polymerization (TPP) is a powerful and potential technology to fabricate true three-dimensional (3D) micro/nanostructures of various materials with subdiffraction-limit resolution. And it has been applied to microoptics, electronics, communications, biomedicine, microfluidic devices, MEMS and metamaterials. These applications, such as microoptics and photon crystals, put forward rigorous requirements on the processing accuracy of TPP, including the dimensional accuracy, shape accuracy a-
nd surface roughness and the processing accuracy influences their performance, even invalidate them. In order to fabricate precise 3D micro/nanostructures, the factors influencing the processing accuracy need to be considered comprehensively and systematically. In this paper, we review the basis of TPP micro/nanofabrication, including mechanism of TPP, experimental set-up for TPP and scaling laws of resolution of TPP. Then, we discuss the factors influencing the processing accuracy. Finally, we su-
mmarize the methods reported lately to improve the processing accuracy from improving the resolution and changing spatial arrangement of voxels. C 2015 Author(s). I. INTRODUCTION
Three dimensional (3D) micro/nanostructures have widespread applications in the field of microoptics, electronics, communications, biomedicine, microfluidic devices, MEMS, metamaterials.
And these fields also put forward higher requirements on the miniaturization and integration of 3D micro/nanostructures, which lead to various micro/nanofabrication technologies like Deep UV lithography,1 electron/ion beam lithography,2,3 NanoImprint Lithography (NIL),4 microstereolithography,5 Ink-jet printing6 and self assembly.7 However, these technologies have encountered many problems inhibiting their actual applications, such as the incapability to fabricate complicated 3D micro/nanostructur-
es, restricted resolution and huge cost. Two-photon polymerization (TPP) is a lay-by-lay method and has great advantages in manufacturing arbitrary three dimensional (3D) micro/nanostructures of various materials such as polymers, hybrid materials, organically modified ceramics (Ormocer), and metals with subdiffraction-limit resolution (down to 100 nm).8–13
Since the theoretical proposition of two-photon absorption (TPA)14 and experimental confirmation,15 TPP has attracted great interests all over the world. Until in 1997, when Maruo et al.16 fabricated
a spiral structure, the diameter of which is about 7 µm, TPP started to be applied to fabricate 3D micro/nanostructures. In 2001, a microbull with feature sizes of 120 nm beyound the diffraction limit (about 390 nm when λ is 780 nm and NA is 1 in Abbe’s diffraction limit formular d = λ/(2NA)) was fabricated.8 It was a historical moment, after which various applications of TPP sprang up, such as photonic crystals,9,17,18 microfluidic device,19–24 biomedical science,11,25,26 microoptics,27–30 dielec-

The factors that influence the processing accuracy include geometry errors of the 3D scanning stages and the principle errors of TPP. TPP is known as a lay-by-lay method and every layer is stacked up by the fundamental element-voxels. Small voxels and tight arrangement are conducive to high accuracy. So the accuracy of the photon-polymerized structures relies on the voxels and their spatial arrangement. In direct laser writing (DLW), resolution and feature size are two different concepts easy to b-
e confused. The former refers to the minimal spacing of two adjacent yet separated structures.34 In TPP fabrication, many scholars used the resolution but actually referred to the feature size. In terms of an isolate voxel, we regard the resolution as the feature size in this paper.

Then, we will discuss the factors influencing the processing accuracy of TPP. Finally we will summarize the principles and achievements of the methods to improve the processing accuracy from improving the resolution and changing voxels’ spatial arrangement.

A. Mechanism of TPP

volume of the photosensitive resins by a high-numerical-aperture (NA) objective. A normal photopolymerization process is described in equations (1), (2), and (3).35 It consists of three processes:
the initiation process, the propagation process and the termination process. In the initiation process, photoinitiators (PIs) reach to the excited state (PI∗) by absorption of two photons and decompose to radicals (R·). In the propagation process, the radicals combine with monomers (M) to produce monomer radicals (RMn·). In the termination process, two monomer radicals are combined and the photo-polymerization process is terminated.

the initiator absorbs two photons with the same frequency. Whereas, in the non-degenerate process, the initiator absorbs two photons with different frequencies. B. Experimental set-up for TPP
The optical system for TPP varies according to the different applications. But the basic components are shared in common. The laser beam comes from a mode-locked Ti: sapphire laser and its power is diminished by an attenuator. Then it is expanded by a laser beam expander to reduce the divergence angle and focused tightly into the volume of the resin by a high-numerical-aperture objective (NA of 1.4 and magnification of 100 times for fabricating fine structures). The exposure time is controlled by -
the shutter. The CMOS/CCD camera enables online process monitoring. The 3D scanning of the laser beam relative to the sample is realized originally by a 3D piezoelectric/PZT stage with high resolution,16 but its travel and scanning speed are limited. Then, many scholars used a two-dimensional galvanometer scanner combined with a one-dimensional piezoelectric/PZT stage to improve the scanning speed.35,39 With the development of high-precision positioning technology, 3D linear motor-driven stage40 a-
nd 3D motor-driven linear stage combined with two-dimensional galvanometer41 were used to fabricate microstructures over a large area reaching to millimeters. In Fig. 2, we summarize these schemes according to the difference of the scanning stage.
C. Resolution of TPP
Resolution is an important parameter representing the feature size of the micro/nanostructures fabricated by TPP. It is relevant to the form of the fundamental unit of TPP, the voxels or lines. Usually, there are two different scanning modes in the fabrication process, the pinpoint scanning (or spot-evaluation) mode and the continuing scanning (or line scanning) mode. The resolution of TPP in the two kinds of scanning modes refer to the feature size of voxels and the width of lines, respectively. -
Many studies have found that the voxel resembles a spinning ellipsoid whose lateral diameter is smaller than the axial length.42 So the feature sizes of voxels include the lateral (d) and axial (l) dimensions. The ratio of axial feature size to lateral one is called aspect ratio (α), that is α = l/d. Many scholars have studied the scaling laws of the resolution of TPP when changing the parameters, such as the power of femtosecond laser, exposure time, NA of the objective, and have made great achie-
vements.

Conclusions
An ascending scan method was utilized to achieve isolated complete 3D voxels. The initiator transformed the quadratic light intensity distribution to a linear radical concentration distribution. Variation of the beam polarization direction relative to the objective led to corresponding orientation rotation of voxels, while their size and shape remain unchanged. A lower NA produced a slimmer voxel. Short exposure time produced a small voxel. simultaneous recording of zeroth- and higher-order diffra-
ction patterns. Increasing laser power brought about slimmer voxel than increasing exposure time because of the laws of Focal Spot Duplication and Voxel Growth they followed. The voxel was asymmetric (aspect ratio of 4:1 or greater) at high dose and nearly spherical at low dose (aspect ratio 1:1 or lower) regardless of the type of polymerization systems.

V. CONCLUSION
TPP as a layer-by-layer technique has great advantages in fabricating arbitrary 3D micro/ nanostructures of various materials with ultra-resolution and it has been applied to optics, electronics, communications, biomedicine, microfluidic devices, MEMS and metamaterials. However, accuracy of the micro/nanostructures fabricated by TPP hinders their actual applications especially in optics. In this review, we focus on the improvement of the processing accuracy from improving the resolution of a isola-
te voxel and changing the spatial arrangement of voxels and introduce efforts to fabricate precise micro/nanostructures. Although great achievements have been made, there is still much work to do. With the demand for large area micro/nanostructures, a long travel stage is needed. As is well known that the accuracy of a long travel stage is worse than the accuracy of a short travel stage. The geometry errors of the stage cannot guarantee exact position and posture of the voxels and bring about the -
position errors and posture errors of the voxels. The errors existing in voxels will affect the processing accuracy of TPP. Therefore, study of the long travel stage with high precision and compensation of these errors is expected in the future. Besides, errors of the STL file format make it no longer suitable for high precise micro/nanofabrication and a new precise file format is needed urgently. Processing accuracy and efficiency are contradictory. Thin layer thickness means high processing accu-
racy, but leads to much processing time. The balance between the accuracy and efficiency needs to be considered in the future. We believe that with these issues solved, TPP will have more applications in ultraprecise fields.
