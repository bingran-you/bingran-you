# Zero-Human Company and One-Person Company: Two Names for the Same Shift

I spent a few months treating these as two different concepts and trying to draw a clean line between them. That line does not exist in the way the terms are actually being used in 2026. Both phrases describe the same organisational shift. About 90% of the meaning overlaps. They are near-synonyms — one points at the human, the other points at the team — and treating them as opposites or as a spectrum of more-vs-less misses the point.

This post is the corrected version of the framing.

## What both phrases name

The same 2024–2026 pattern: a single founder running a complete business — product, marketing, sales, support, ops — with an AI labour stack that did not exist as a coherent thing before. Coding agents (Claude Code, Codex, OpenClaw, Cursor) handle engineering. Specialised research, growth, support, and operations agents handle the rest. SaaS and APIs are rails. Paperclip-shaped control planes manage the agents as an organisation.

The big question that triggered the new vocabulary was the same in both phrasings: *what does it mean to run a real business when the team is software?*

## Where each phrase puts the emphasis

[One-person company](/one-person-company) puts the emphasis on the human. *One founder runs this.* It travels with stories about specific founders — Matthew Gallagher and Medvi, Sam Altman&apos;s and Dario Amodei&apos;s public predictions of the first one-person billion-dollar company, the Chinese policy programmes in Shenzhen, Hangzhou, and Beijing, Coinbase&apos;s &quot;one-person teams&quot; experiment inside a public company.

[Zero-human company](/zero-human-company) puts the emphasis on the team. *No employees execute the day-to-day work.* It travels with stories about agent-organisation infrastructure — Paperclip as the canonical open-source control plane, Andon Market&apos;s Luna running a physical SF retail store, Anthropic&apos;s Project Vend, ClawBank&apos;s Manfred self-incorporating an LLC, Coinbase x402 and AWS Bedrock AgentCore Payments as agentic commerce rails.

These are two angles on the same picture. Pick whichever phrase fits the conversation; the underlying thing being described is the same.

## Why the conflation hurts when you treat them as opposites

If you treat the two phrases as a spectrum (one-person at the low end, zero-human at the high end), you end up insisting on a difference that the actual usage does not support. Coinbase&apos;s &quot;one-person teams&quot; memo and Paperclip&apos;s &quot;zero-human company&quot; tagline are pointing at the same kind of thing. So are Sam Altman&apos;s solo-billion prediction and the Andon Market experiment. The difference is which side of the picture is in focus, not which side of a spectrum you are on.

If you treat them as a binary opposition (one-person = founder still does work, zero-human = founder gone), you misread both ends. The founder is never gone in any 2026 implementation — even Andon Market explicitly has Andon Labs as the responsible organisation and human staff for physical work. And the founder of an &quot;ordinary&quot; one-person company in 2026 is rarely doing line-level execution either; the agents are.

## A working vocabulary, restated

For this site, I will use the terms as follows:

- **[One-person company](/one-person-company)** — the founder-emphasising name for the 2024–2026 pattern: one human orchestrating an AI labour stack to run a complete business. Travels with predictions, reference cases, founder workflow, policy context.
- **[Zero-human company](/zero-human-company)** — the team-emphasising name for the same pattern: no employees execute the day-to-day work. Travels with agent-organisation control planes (Paperclip), live experiments (Andon Market, Project Vend), legal/financial rails (ClawBank), payment rails (x402, AgentCore Payments).
- **Solopreneur** — a separate, older idea: one human personally does the execution work. Distinct because no agent layer; the bottleneck is hours.

If you are pitching, building, hiring, or critiquing in this space, picking the right emphasis matters. Trying to police the line between &quot;one-person&quot; and &quot;zero-human&quot; does not — they are two names for the same thing.
