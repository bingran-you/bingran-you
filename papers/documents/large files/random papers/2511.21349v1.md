MULTIPLICITY OF SOLUTIONS FOR GROSS-PITAEVSKII EQUATIONS ON RIEMANNIAN MANIFOLDS
DARIO CORONA, STEFANO NARDULLI, RAMON OLIVER-BONAFOUX, AND GIANDOMENICO ORLANDI
Abstract. We provide a multiplicity result for solutions of time-independent GrossPitaevskii equations on closed Riemannian manifolds. Such solutions arise as (possibly non-minimizing) critical points of the Ginzburg-Landau energy having prescribed momentum according to a given tangent velocity field. Lower bounds on the multiplicity of solutions are obtained in terms of the topology of the maximum velocity set, in the small momentum and vorticity core size regime. The proof relies on methods from-
 critical point theory and Γ–convergence for Ginzburg-Landau functionals as well as on some new results for codimension 2 isoperimetric-type problems in the small flux regime, possibly of independent interest.

Furthermore, such small discs should be centered around a point in which the modulus of X is maximal so that the perimeter is minimized. In this direction, a contribution of this paper, possibly of independent interest, is to provide a proof of the fact that almost minimizers of our isoperimetric problem have almost all of their vol-
ume concentrated around a geodesic disk close to a point where X maximizes its norm. Let us also point out that, in contrast with isoperimetric problems in codimension 1, their higher-dimensional analogues seem to have been much less explored so far. Moreover, to our knowledge, only the works [47,54] consider the notion of volume weighted by a given vector field X as we do here. The classical paper by Almgren [12] and more recent works (e.g., Mazzeo, Pacard and Zolotareva [45]) take a different no-
tion of “enclosed volume” of higher codimension (namely, the minimal volume of all the submanifolds which share the same boundary). Flux-constrained Ginzburg-Landau-type problems and their link with higher-dimensional isoperimetric problems also seem to have remained quite unexplored so far: to our knowledge, besides the present paper, it appears only on [26, 27] as well as on different but related work by Roma ́n, Sandier and Serfaty [52, 53]. 3. Outline of the proof
The proof of Theorem 1 is based on the so-called photography method, a technique in critical point theory introduced by Benci and Cerami [17] (see also [16, Section IV.7]). We recall that given a C1 functional E on a smooth Banach manifold M and a sequence (xn)n∈N ⊂ M, the latter is said to be a Palais-Smale sequence for E when (E(xn))n∈N is bounded and (∥dE(xn)∥(TxnM)∗)n∈N tends to zero as n tends to 0. In this setting, the functional is said to satisfy the Palais-Smale condition when every Palai-
s-Smale sequence possesses a strongly convergent subsequence.
Theorem 3.1 (Photography method, cf. [16, 17]). Let X be a topological space, M be a C2-Hilbert manifold, and let E : M → R be a C1-functional. For any c ∈ R, define the sublevel set
Ec := {x ∈ M : E(x) ≤ c}.
Assume the following conditions hold:
(1) infx∈M E(x) > −∞;
(2) E satisfies the Palais–Smale (PS) condition; (3) There exist two continuous maps f : X → Ec and β : Ec → X such that β ◦ f is homotopic to the identity map of X.
Then, the number of critical points of E in Ec is at least cat(X). Moreover, if M is contractible and cat(X) > 1, then there exists at least one additional critical point of E outside Ec. Furthermore, if the number of critical points is finite, there exists c0 ∈ (c, ∞) such that Ec contains exactly P1(X) critical points, and if X is contractible, then Ec0 \ Ec contains P1(X) − 1 critical points, counted with multiplicity.
The name “photography” reflects that f (X) may be regarded as faithful picture of X inside M. Indeed, the retraction β : Ec → Z with β◦f ≃ idX, one can recognize X inside the sublevel Ec; in particular, homotopy-invariant quantities of X (such as cat(X), cup-length, the Betti numbers) transfer to Ec. This passage of topology is precisely what underlies the lower bounds on the number of critical points. Beyond the previously mentioned applications of the photography method in the study of the Allen-
-Cahn functional (see [13,14,18–20,32]), this technique has also been employed in various other contexts. For instance, Cingolani and Lazzo [29, 30] used it to analyze standing waves of a nonlinear Schr ̈odinger equation. More recently, Petean [50] applied it to establish a multiplicity result for the Yamabe equation, while Alarc ́on, Petean and Rey [7] investigated its role in the study of conformal metrics with constant Q-curvature.
