## Fixed pages for the site

With the PDF of his paper open in Chrome's viewer, Bingran asked: "the layout of the whole page, should be fully follow this style ... how how you move between pages and what font to use / make sure all the style is pixel level mock but do not mention any thing about \"Nature\" in the template. just keep the style would be fine / check the nature's restriction to make sure we do not violate their policies".

What was built (the procedure is in `personal-site/README.md`):

- The home page is typeset at authoring time. `npm run typeset` drives headless Chrome on a development-only proof view, reads back where each line breaks, paginates, and writes `content/layout/home.json` (geometry only). The pages are a display list of frozen lines.
- The layout carries a hash of the content and of everything it was typeset by. A stale layout fails `next build` and a test. Vercel then keeps the previous deployment, so a content edit without `npm run typeset` cannot reach production half-set.
- From 900 px up a page is laid out at one size and scaled as a whole. Below that the same content is one fluid column.
- `/about`, `/projects`, `/papers`: one fixed page each. `/posts`: still one tall sheet. A pager that packed the cards onto pages in the browser was built and removed again: one post is taller than a page and a third of the pages came out half empty.

Fonts, chosen by measurement: about 130 open families were compared with the printed page by per-word width, x-height, stem weight and letter overlap. Roboto Serif (with its width and optical-size axes) is closest for every serif style and Inter for the sans; Noto Serif supplies Greek. The fitted size, weight, width and spacing of each style are tokens at the top of `components/article/article.module.css`.

Policy check (research by five agents with a source check, not legal advice):

- The publisher has no published rule about imitating a page layout. Its terms restrict the name and logo, its own page furniture (running foot, DOI and received/accepted block, the update badge), framing its pages, its PDF and typeset figures. Its typefaces are custom and cannot be licensed.
- So: the journal's name stays out of the template and only in the citation of his paper; nothing is copied from the publisher; text and figures come from the arXiv versions; the two papers on the publisher's site link through the DOI resolver; Creative Commons credits link to the licence.
- An exact replica is where the sources run out. A certain answer could only come from the publisher's permissions team or a lawyer.

Known limits of the paged layout, accepted for now:

- The content is mostly figures, so the tail is loose: the text and the reference list end part-way down page 6 and page 7 holds the last two figures.
- In windows between 900 and 1297 px on a non-retina screen, hairlines are slightly soft because the page is scaled.
- A browser minimum font size of 16 px, or blocked web fonts, makes some lines overrun their column.
- Checked in Chrome (headless, every width) and by eye in Safari. Firefox is not installed here and was not run.

## Things worth knowing next time

- The Discord invite `x2KYk4FVS` resolves today but carries an expiry of 2026-11-09. A permanent invite is needed before then.
- `rm` is refused by the permission layer in agent runs here; `trash` works. About 66 MB of removed palace files sit in the macOS Trash.
- The dev server for this worktree does not survive long idle periods. Check that port 3210 answers before handing an agent a URL.
- `python3 -I` cannot import PyMuPDF on this machine (it lives in the user site directory). `python3 -P` keeps the script directory off the path and still finds it.
- A dependency-free headless-Chrome driver that is killed with SIGKILL leaves Chrome's helper processes running. After a long measurement run, look for `--user-data-dir=.../cdp-*` processes and stop them.
- Vercel skips the preview of a new branch whose last commit does not touch `personal-site/`. Keep the journal in the same commit as the code, or commit it first.
- Codex writes its model pick into `~/.codex/config.toml` itself. The `gpt-6.1-sol` there on 2026-10-10 was his own last selection in the app, not a stale default, so it was left alone.
