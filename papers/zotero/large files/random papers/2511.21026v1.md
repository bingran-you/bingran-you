SPECTRAL THEORY AND ALMOST PERIODIC STRUCTURES IN
HOM–LIE BANACH ALGEBRAS
MARWA ENNACEUR
Abstract. We develop a systematic functional-analytic framework for Hom–Lie Banach algebras, introducing bounded α-twisted derivations and almost periodic elements. Under natural continuity and compactness assumptions, we establish a complete Bohr–Fourier spectral decomposition of such derivations. We prove that the associated almost periodic and ergodic subspaces are not merely topological complements but closed, α-invariant subalgebras, stable under the twisted Lie bracket—a key structural novel-
ty that enables coherent restriction of the dynamics. We provide explicit constructions of Hom–Banach–Malcev algebras and demonstrate our theory with concrete operator-algebraic applications, including a novel twisted Weyl algebra example, analyzed via the metaplectic representation, where a non-commuting twist enriches the Bohr spectrum from a cyclic group to a two-dimensional lattice.

1. Introduction
The theory of Hom–Lie algebras, initially introduced by Hartwig, Larsson, and Silvestrov [7] in the context of σ-deformations of Witt and Virasoro algebras, has since evolved into a vibrant branch of non-associative algebra. Extensions to Hom–Malcev algebras [14] and Hom–Jordan structures have further broadened its scope. However, most existing work remains confined to purely algebraic or finite-dimensional settings, often lacking the analytical tools needed to address dynamical or spectral questi-
ons. In contrast, this paper initiates a systematic study of Hom–Lie Banach algebras—infinitedimensional, topologically complete non-associative algebras equipped with a bounded twisting map α and a twisted Lie bracket [a, b]α = α(ab − ba). Our framework integrates three major strands of modern mathematics: (i) the algebraic flexibility of Hom-type structures [8, 14], (ii) the functional-analytic machinery of Banach algebras and C∗-dynamics [9], and (iii) the harmonicanalytic theory of almost peri-
odic functions in Banach spaces [1, 10]. Our main contributions are as follows:
• Spectral decomposition of twisted derivations. We establish a full Bohr–Fourier spectral decomposition for bounded inner α-twisted derivations δ = adα(X), under the natural assumption that the induced C0-group T (t) = etδ has relatively compact orbits. This extends the classical spectral theory of almost periodic representations (see Namioka [10]) to the non-associative Hom–Lie context. Crucially, we demonstrate that the twisting map

2. Preliminaries
2.1. Banach algebras and derivations. Let A be a complex Banach algebra with associative product denoted by juxtaposition (a, b) 7→ ab and norm ∥ · ∥. A bounded linear map D : A → A is called a derivation if
D(ab) = D(a)b + aD(b) for all a, b ∈ A.
2.2. Hom–Lie algebras and twisted brackets.
Definition 2.1. A Hom–Lie algebra (L, [·, ·]α, α) consists of a complex vector space L, a linear map α : L → L (the twisting map), and a bilinear bracket [·, ·]α : L × L → L satisfying:
(1) [x, y]α = −[y, x]α (skew-symmetry); (2) The Hom–Jacobi identity:
[α(x), [y, z]α]α + [α(y), [z, x]α]α + [α(z), [x, y]α]α = 0
for all x, y, z ∈ L.
When L is a Banach space and both α and [·, ·]α are continuous, we call (L, [·, ·]α, α) a Hom–Lie Banach algebra.
Remark 2.2. In all applications considered in this paper, the Hom–Lie bracket arises from the associative product of a Banach algebra A via the canonical commutator:
[a, b] := ab − ba, [a, b]α := α([a, b]) = α(ab − ba), a, b ∈ A.
This construction ensures compatibility between the algebraic and topological structures. In this setting, inner α-twisted derivations are given by
adα(X)(a) = [X, a]α = α(Xa − aX), X, a ∈ A.
This convention is assumed throughout Sections 4–5.

2.3. Twisted derivations.
Definition 2.3. Let (A, ·) be a Banach algebra and α : A → A a bounded linear map. An α-twisted derivation is a bounded linear operator D : A → A satisfying
D(ab) = D(a) α(b) + α(a) D(b) for all a, b ∈ A.
If there exists X ∈ A such that
D(a) = [X, a]α for all a ∈ A,
where [·, ·]α is the Hom–Lie bracket defined above, then D is said to be inner, and we write D = adα (X ).
3. Hom–Banach–Malcev algebras
Definition 3.1. A Hom–Banach–Malcev algebra (M, [·, ·]α, α) is a complex Banach space (M, ∥·∥) equipped with a bounded linear map α : M → M and a continuous bilinear bracket [·, ·]α : M×M → M such that
[x, y]α = α([x, y]) for all x, y ∈ M,
where [·, ·] is a continuous Malcev bracket on M (i.e., skew-symmetric and satisfying the Malcev identity), and the following Hom–Malcev identity holds:
Jα(α(x), α(y), [x, z]α) = [Jα(x, y, z), α2(x)]α,
where
Jα(x, y, z) := [α(x), [y, z]α]α + [α(y), [z, x]α]α + [α(z), [x, y]α]α
is the Hom–Jacobiator. This identity coincides with the standard Hom–Malcev condition introduced by Yau [14, Def. 2.1].
Lemma 3.2. If the underlying Malcev bracket satisfies ∥[x, y]∥ ≤ C∥x∥∥y∥ for some C > 0 and α is bounded, then the twisted bracket [·, ·]α is continuous and satisfies
∥[x, y]α∥ ≤ C∥α∥∥x∥∥y∥ for all x, y ∈ M.
Proof. By definition, [x, y]α = α([x, y]). Hence
∥[x, y]α∥ = ∥α([x, y])∥ ≤ ∥α∥ ∥[x, y]∥ ≤ C∥α∥∥x∥∥y∥,
which proves continuity. □
Theorem 3.3. Suppose (M, [·, ·]) is a Banach–Malcev algebra and α : M → M is a bounded linear map that is also a Malcev algebra morphism, i.e.,
α([x, y]) = [α(x), α(y)] for all x, y ∈ M.
Then (M, [·, ·]α, α) satisfies the Hom–Malcev identity.
Proof. Because α is a Malcev morphism, the twisted bracket can be written as [x, y]α = [α(x), α(y)]. A direct calculation then shows that the Hom–Jacobiator is related to the classical Jacobiator J(x, y, z) := [x, [y, z]] + [y, [z, x]] + [z, [x, y]] by
Jα(x, y, z) = [α(x), [α(y), α(z)]] + [α(y), [α(z), α(x)]] + [α(z), [α(x), α(y)]] = J(α(x), α(y), α(z)).
Applying the classical Malcev identity to the triple (α(x), α(y), [α(x), α(z)]) yields
J(α(x), α(y), [α(x), α(z)]) = [[α(x), α(y)], α(x)].
Using the morphism property, [α(x), α(z)] = α([x, z]) = [x, z]α and [α(x), α(y)] = [x, y]α. Substituting these expressions gives
Jα(α(x), α(y), [x, z]α) = [[x, y]α, α(x)].
Finally, since Jα(x, y, z) = α(J(x, y, z)) (as α is a morphism), we have
[[x, y]α, α(x)] = [α(J(x, y, z)), α(x)] = [Jα(x, y, z), α2(x)]α,
which is the desired Hom–Malcev identity. □

Proposition 3.6. If α is an isometric Malcev algebra automorphism, then (M, [·, ·]α, α) is a Hom–Banach–Malcev algebra and ∥[x, y]α∥ = ∥[x, y]∥ for all x, y ∈ M.
Proof. Since α is an automorphism, it is a Malcev morphism, so the Hom–Malcev identity holds by Theorem 3.3. Isometry gives ∥[x, y]α∥ = ∥α([x, y])∥ = ∥[x, y]∥. □

5. Applications and Examples
We now illustrate the abstract framework with three concrete classes of Hom–Lie Banach algebras. In each case, the bracket is defined by [a, b]α = α(ab − ba), the twisting map α is bounded (often isometric), and the inner derivation δ = adα(X) is bounded.
5.1. Shift Operators. Let A = B(l2(Z)), the Banach algebra of bounded linear operators on l2(Z), equipped with the operator norm and composition product. Let U denote the bilateral shift operator, (U ξ)n = ξn+1, and define the inner automorphism
α(T ) = U T U ∗, T ∈ B(l2(Z)).
Then α is an isometric ∗-automorphism. Setting [T, S] = T S − ST and [T, S]α = α([T, S]), the triple (A, [·, ·]α, α) becomes a Hom–Lie Banach algebra. For any self-adjoint X ∈ A, the map δ = adα(X) is a bounded linear operator and generates a uniformly bounded C0-group T (t) = etδ. If X belongs to the C∗-algebra of almost periodic operators—for instance, if X = Mφ is a Laurent (multiplication) operator with almost periodic symbol φ—then the orbit {T (t)T : t ∈ R} is relatively compact for every fi-
nite-rank operator T ∈ A. Moreover, if X is shift-invariant (e.g., a convolution operator), then α(X) = X, which implies αδ = δα, so Assumption 4.3 holds.
Example 5.1. Let α(T ) = U T U ∗ with U the bilateral shift on l2(Z), and let X = Mφ be multiplication by φ(n) = eiθn for some θ ∈ R. Then X is normal, δ = adα(X) is bounded, and for any finiterank T , the orbit {T (t)T }t∈R is relatively compact. The Bohr spectrum is σap(δ) = {ikθ : k ∈ Z}, and the spectral decomposition of Theorem 4.2 recovers the classical Fourier series of φ.

6. Morphisms and Invariant Substructures
To place our constructions in a categorical context, we introduce a natural notion of morphism between Hom–Lie Banach algebras.
Definition 6.1. Let (A, [·, ·]α, α) and (B, [·, ·]β, β) be Hom–Lie Banach algebras. A bounded linear map φ : A → B is a Hom–Lie morphism if it satisfies
φ ◦ α = β ◦ φ and φ([x, y]α) = [φ(x), φ(y)]β for all x, y ∈ A.

7. Technical Remarks on Convergence and Limitations
We conclude with a technical clarification on convergence and a discussion of current limitations, accompanied by concrete pathways for generalization.
Lemma 7.1. Let A be a Banach space with the bounded approximation property (e.g., any C∗algebra, B(H), or lp for 1 ≤ p < ∞). If a series P
λ∈Λ aλ in A satisfies P
λ ∥aλ∥ < ∞, then it converges unconditionally in norm. In particular, the spectral decomposition in Theorem 4.2 is independent of the enumeration of the Bohr spectrum σap(δ).
Proof. This is a standard consequence of the Orlicz–Pettis theorem (see, e.g., Diestel–Uhl, Vector Measures, Thm. IV.10). All spaces appearing in our applications—C∗-algebras, operator algebras, and sequence spaces—possess the bounded approximation property, so the conclusion applies universally in our setting. □
Remark 7.2 (Limitations and Concrete Extensions). The requirement that all orbits {T (t)a : t ∈ R} be relatively compact is restrictive: it excludes physically relevant scenarios such as dissipative quantum systems, open dynamics, or chaotic (hyperbolic) evolutions. Likewise, the boundedness of the twisted derivation δ precludes natural unbounded examples—e.g., differential twisted derivations on algebras of smooth functions or Sobolev spaces. We propose the following concrete avenues for generali-
zation:
(1) Unbounded twisted derivations. A natural extension is to replace the bounded operator δ with a closed, densely defined twisted derivation D : Dom(D) ⊂ A → A. In this setting, the C0-group T (t) would be generated via the Hille–Yosida theorem. For the spectral decomposition to persist, one would require that D generates an analytic or Gevrey-regular semi-group, ensuring sufficient smoothness of the orbits t 7→ T (t)a for a ∈ Dom(D∞). This would connect our work to the foundational theories of S-
tone, Hille–Yosida, and Kato on unbounded operators, and to the framework of ∗-algebras of unbounded operators (see, e.g., Schmüdgen [12] or Woronowicz [13]). (2) Weaker notions of almost periodicity. The assumption of norm-almost periodicity can be relaxed to Besicovitch almost periodicity in the B2-space, where convergence is understood in the mean-square sense. This would involve replacing the Bohr–Fourier coefficients with Cesàro means and would yield a spectral decomposition in a weaker topol-
ogy. Such an approach is standard in the study of non-compact dynamical systems and would link our results to the ergodic theory of von Neumann algebras (cf. Connes [2]). (3) Spectral radius and Gelfand theory. Our dynamical approach could be fruitfully combined with the algebraic spectral theory of El Kinani–Ben Ali [4]. Their notion of a HomGelfand spectrum provides a complementary, purely algebraic perspective. A key open question is to relate the dynamical Bohr spectrum σap(adα(X)) to the alge-
braic Hom–Gelfand spectrum of X itself, potentially via a Hom–version of the spectral mapping theorem.
