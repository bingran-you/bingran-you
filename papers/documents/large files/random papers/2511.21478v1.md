Markov properties for the vertical edge profile in random
labelled trees
Alexis Metz-Donnadieu
November 27, 2025
Abstract
We study a broad class of random labelled trees in which integer-valued labels evolve along the edges according to increments in {−1, 0, 1}. These models include e.g. branching random walks, embedded complete and incomplete binary trees, random Cayley and plane trees with uniform displacements along edges. Motivated by recent work suggesting a Markovian structure in the vertical profile of such trees, we introduce the vertical edge profile, which counts both oriented edges connecting label k − 1 t-
o label k and oriented edges connecting label k to label k − 1. We show that the vertical edge profile forms a time-homogeneous Markov chain for a wide class of models, and this remains true (provided we enrich this process by the total mass of the tree below each label) if we condition on the total size of the tree. We give explicit transition kernels in the case of labelled incomplete binary trees, which are closely related to known enumeration formulas. To establish these results, we study a de-
composition of labelled trees into excursions above and below fixed label levels, yielding a forest structure with tractable probabilistic laws. We further explain briefly how these findings connect to the theory of super-Brownian motion and the Integrated Super-Brownian Excursion (ISE). In a companion paper, we show that the vertical edge profile converges—after rescaling—to the local time and its derivative of Brownian motion indexed by the Brownian tree.

These models of labelled trees have become staple objects in probability theory and have been extensively studied both in combinatorics [4, 7], and in probability and random geometry [2, 10, 12], motivated b-
y numerous applications to other models in statistical physics [11, 21, 24].
In this paper, we are interested in a process closely related to the so-called vertical profile of random labelled trees. The vertical profile of a labelled tree (t, lt) is the family of nonnegative integers (xt(k))k∈Z, where for every k ∈ Z, xt(k) denotes the number of vertices in (t, lt) with label k. For a randomly sampled tree, the vertical profile describes the (random) distribution of vertex labels, and can be interpreted as the occupation measure of the branching process encoded by the rand-
om labelled tree. In particular, some work has been done in trying to understand the asymptotic properties of the vertical profile when the size of the tree is conditioned to grow to infinity. Under the assumption that the offspring distribution is critical and the displacement laws are centred, it has been shown in various cases [5, 13] that the vertical profile converges (after suitable rescaling) to a random, differentiable function fISE. This function is the density of the Integrated SuperBrow-
nian Excursion, a random measure first introduced by Aldous in [2]. As the name suggests, this density is connected to the study of the local times of the superBrownian motion—a random measure-valued stochastic processthat has attracted interest in past decades [15, 23, 26]. Recent work has aimed to better understand the distributional properties of the random function fISE and related processes. These investigations use both continuous methods (such as e.g. excursion theory for Brownian motion in-
dexed by the Brownian tree [1, 14]) as well as discrete/combinatorial methods [4, 5, 19].
In a recent work by Chapuy and Marckert [8], the authors study the uniform naturally embedded incomplete binary tree model and show that the 3D process formed by the vertical profile, its discrete derivative, and its discrete integral behaves—when properly conditioned—like a Markov chain. Based on this, they conjectured that the limiting process fISE, together with its derivative and integral, forms a Markov process governed by an SDE. This conjecture was confirmed for a closely related process in-
 [17, 18]. The results in [8] only work for the binary tree model, as they relied heavily on explicit enumeration formulas for binary trees with prescribed vertical profile, established in [7] by Chapuy and Bousquet-Mélou. Nonetheless, these results suggest that the vertical profile could be studied effectively via Markov chain methods, an insight that partly motivated the present work. 2 Preliminaries
2.1 General notations
Let us begin by introducing some general notations. A rooted tree T is a connected, acyclic graph on a finite vertex set V (T ), with a distinguished vertex ρT ∈ V (T ) called the root of T . We say that T is a (rooted) plane tree if in addition, for each vertex v ∈ V (T ), a linear order is specified on the set of its children in T —that is, the (possibly empty) set of vertices adjacent to v that are farther from ρT than v with respect to the graph distance in T . For each v ∈ V (T ), we write:
• kT (v) for the number of children of v,
• πT (v) for the parent of v in T , defined only if v ̸= ρT .
By convention, the edges of T are oriented away from the root—that is, from parents to children. This orientation induces a partial order ⪯ on the vertices of T , where u ⪯ v if and only if u lies on the ancestral path from the root to v. We write u ≺ v to mean u ⪯ v and u ̸= v. In the following, the subtree branching off a vertex v is defined as the subtree of T rooted at v, consisting of all descendants of v in T —that is, the subtree induced by all vertices u ∈ V (T ) such that v ⪯ u. In this w-
ork, we are interested in models of labelled rooted plane trees, defined as pairs (T, lT ), where T is a rooted plane tree and lT : V (T ) → Z is an integer-valued function assigning a label to each vertex of T , subject to the constraint:
∀v ∈ V (T ) \ {ρT }, lT (v) − lT (πT (v)) ∈ {−1, 0, 1}.
These definitions naturally extend to random labelled forests as well—that is, finite sequences of labelled rooted plane trees. In the following, we write T for the set of all labelled plane trees. If t ∈ T, we will (somewhat abusively) keep the same notation t to talk about the associated rooted plane tree (so that the notation V (t), ρt, kt(v), πt(v) · · · still make sense) and we will always write lt : V (t) → Z for the associated labelling function. A planar map is an equivalence class (under orientation-preserving homeomorphisms of the sphere) of finite graphs embedded in the sphere. Multiple edges are allowed, and the boundary of a face need not be a simple cycle. We focus here on pointed quadrangulations, which are planar maps q satisfying the following properties:
• Every face of q has degree 4 (equivalently, the dual graph is 4-valent).
• The map is rooted—that is, a distinguished oriented edge e is specified. We denote by x0 and x1 the origin and endpoint of e, respectively.
• The map is pointed—that is, it has a distinguished vertex x⋆ ∈ V (q), which may or may not coincide with x0 or x1.
