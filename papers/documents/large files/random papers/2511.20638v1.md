ON THE C∗-ALGEBRAS OF LINEAR DYNAMICAL SYSTEMS
INGRID BELTIŢĂ AND DANIEL BELTIŢĂ
Abstract. We verify the conjecture on continuous-trace subquotients for C∗algebras of nilpotent linear dynamical systems, where by linear dynamical system we mean a continuous action of the additive group of real numbers by linear maps on a finite-dimensional real vector space. In addition, we show that the dimension of the ambient vector space can be recovered from the corresponding C∗-algebra and, if the action is nilpotent of degree two, the corresponding group is C∗-rigid within the class of 1-
-connected nilpotent Lie groups with coadjoint orbits of dimension ≤ 2.
1. Introduction
In this note, by linear dynamical system we mean a continuous action of the additive group of real numbers R by linear maps on a finite-dimensional real vector space V. These actions can be given via linear operators on V, namely an operator D ∈ End R(V) corresponds to the group action αD : R × V → V, (t, v) 7→ etDv. A structure of that type gives rise to the semidirect product group GD := V ⋊αD R. This is a solvable Lie group with its corresponding C∗-algebra C∗(GD). As shown in [BB18a], several -
spectral properties of the operator D are reflected in approximation properties of C∗(GD), which suggests the problem of recovering the data (V, D) from the C∗-algebra C∗(GD). That problem requires the study of the fine structure of C∗(GD) encoded by its subquotients. To this end, one needs an alternative description of that C∗algebra in a framework that is more general than the group C∗-algebras. For, the subquotients of C∗(GD) fail to be group C∗-algebras in general, while many of them are C∗-al-
gebras associated to transformation groups defined by the actions of R on suitable subsets of the dual vector space V∗. We are thus naturally lead to working within the framework of groupoid C∗-algebras. Specifically, one has the transformation-group Lie groupoid GD := R × V∗ ⇒ V∗, which in turn gives rise to the groupoid C∗-algebra C∗(GD) that is ∗-isomorphic to the group C∗-algebra C∗(GD), cf. [Re80], [Wi19], and also [BB18b]. More generally, for a solvable Lie group G, the structure of the cota-
ngent groupoid of G, is closely related to the fine structure of the subquotients of C∗(G), as seen in [BB18b]. This new perspective leads to some progress both in the above recovery problem and the following conjecture.
Conjecture 1.1 ([RaRo88, Conj. 4.18]). If G is an exponential solvable Lie group (e.g., a 1-connected nilpotent Lie group) and J1 ⊆ J2 are closed two-sided ideals of C∗(G) whose quotient J2/J1 is a C∗-algebra with continuous trace, then J2/J1 is Morita-equivalent to a commutative C∗-algebra.

3. Subquotients and generalized controlled boundary extensions
Extensions of C∗-algebras and continuous-trace subquotients. Here is a more general version of [BBL17, Lemma 6.5].
Lemma 3.1. Let X be a first-countable topological space with X = X1 ⊔ X2, where the following condition is satisfied:
• If x = {xj}j∈N is a convergent sequence contained in X1, with its set of limit points L(x), then the set L(x) ∩ X2 has no isolated points.
Then for every locally closed and Hausdorff subset S of X the set S ∩X1 is relatively closed in S.
Proof. Let y ∈ S ∩ S ∩ X1. We must prove that y ∈ S ∩ X1. Assume the contrary, that is, y ̸∈ S ∩ X1, hence y ∈ S ∩ X2. Since y ∈ S ∩ X1 and X is a first-countable topological space, there exists a convergent sequence x = {xj}j∈N contained in S ∩ X1, with y ∈ L(x), hence y ∈ L(x) ∩ S ∩ X2. The set S is locally closed, hence there are D ⊂ X open and F ⊂ X closed with S = F ∩ D. Since y ∈ S ⊂ D, we obtain y ∈ (L(x) ∩ X2) ∩ D. By hypothesis, the set L(x) ∩ X2 has no isolated points, hence there is y′ -
∈ (L(x) ∩ X2) ∩ D with y′ ̸= y, and thus
{y, y′} ⊆ (L(x) ∩ X2) ∩ D.
On the other hand x ⊆ S ⊆ F , where we recall that F is closed, and then L(x) ⊆ F . We thus obtain that {y, y′} ⊆ L(x) ∩ D ∩ F = L(x) ∩ S, which is a contradiction with the assumption that S has the Hausdorff property. Consequently we must have y ∈ S ∩ X1, and this completes the proof. □

C∗-rigidity for low-dimensional coadjoint orbits.
Proposition 4.3. Let V0 be a finite-dimensional real vector space, D0 ∈ End (V0) with (D0)2 = 0, and GD0 := V0 ⋊αD0 R. Then the nilpotent Lie group GD0 is C∗-rigid within the class of 1-connected nilpotent Lie groups with coadjoint orbits of dimension ≤ 2.

3. G = N5,4, where dim N5,4 = 5.

This implies that the complement of the set of characters in the unitary dual GdD is not Hausdorff. However, the group GD0 is 2-step nilpotent of class T in the sense of [BB21, Def. 5.1]. Therefore, by the argument in the proof of [BB21, Eq. (5.1)], the complement of the set of characters in the unitary dual GdD0 is Hausdor-
ff. Since the assumption C∗(GD) = C∗(G) ≃ C∗(GD0 ) leads to a homeomorphism GdD ≃ GdD0 , we thus obtained a contradiction. Consequently D2 = 0, and this completes the proof. □
